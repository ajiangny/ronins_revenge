# Shadow Blade: Ronin vs. Samurai — Assignment 1

A Space Invaders remake re-themed as a lone chibi ronin holding a moonlit temple courtyard against marching samurai armies.

Built on the class template ([Oren-Steinberg/Space-Invaders-3D](https://github.com/Oren-Steinberg/Space-Invaders-3D)), Unity 6000.6, URP.
Open `Assets/Scenes/SampleScene.unity` and press Play.

**Controls:** Left/Right or A/D to move (in the temple, WASD or the arrows walk anywhere and E uses a station), Space or Left Click to slash with the katana, W or Up for the Spirit Shuriken, Shift, Down or Right Click to parry, Esc or P to pause. Menus: Up/Down (or W/S) to select, Left/Right to adjust a slider, Enter or Space to confirm, Esc to go back; the mouse and a gamepad work too. On the boon screen: Up/Down (or W/S, or 1/2/3) to select, Enter or click to choose, R to redraw and B to banish once the Flute Songs for them are learned. Between rounds: walk under a portal and press Space, W/Up, Enter or E to step in. Enter or Space skips a boss's intro. For testing (Editor and development builds, and release builds while the GameManager's "Dev Tools In Release" box is ticked, as it is for friends' play-test builds), DEV TOOLS on the title and pause menus (or F3) starts any stage or boss fight directly and toggles god mode (also F4); F1 clears the current army, F2 jumps to the last boss, F5 drops a soul (Fire, Frost and Storm in turn), F6 drops a technique scroll, and F8 prints the balancing run log (also written to `runlog.txt` in the game's data folder).

The template's Space Invaders classes, prefabs and layers are renamed to the theme: `Alien` → `Samurai`, `AlienFleet` → `SamuraiArmy`, `AlienBullet` → `Arrow`, `AlienCounter` → `SamuraiCounter`, `Spaceship` → `Ronin`, `Dart` → `Kunai`, `ShieldWall`/`ShieldBlock` → `BlockerWall`/`Blocker`. The section on template code issues below uses the template's original names.

## Required features

| Requirement | Where |
|---|---|
| Remaining aliens speed up as others are destroyed | `SamuraiArmy.CurrentSpeed()` scales linearly from `baseSpeed` to `baseSpeed × maxSpeedMultiplier` as the army shrinks. |
| Detect when the game can no longer be won | `SamuraiArmy.FixedUpdate()` raises `ArmyReachedRonin` once the lowest samurai reaches the top of the ronin (`Ronin.TopY`). At that height the samurai can't be hit, so `GameManager` ends the game. |
| Ship can't move offscreen | `Ronin.FixedUpdate()` clamps the ronin to the camera's visible width, using his sprite's measured bounds (the pivot isn't centred on the art). |
| Balance (harder but beatable) | See [Balancing](#balancing). |

## Code issues found in the template and fixed

1. **Dynamic Rigidbodies were moved by writing `transform.position` every physics step.** This fights the physics engine and teleports colliders. Aliens and the ship are now kinematic and move with `Rigidbody.MovePosition` (interpolated). Darts and alien bullets are launched once with `linearVelocity`.
2. **Projectiles destroyed whatever they hit (`Destroy(coll.gameObject)`).** An alien bullet would delete the ship, a shield, or anything else outright, which made lives and shields impossible to add. Now each object handles its own collision: `Alien` dies, `Spaceship` loses a life, `ShieldBlock` takes damage, and the projectile only destroys itself. `Dart.TryConsume()` keeps one dart from killing two aliens in the same step.
3. **The UI was polled and rebuilt every frame.** `AlienFleet.Update` copied the count into `AlienCounter`, and `AlienCounter.Update` rebuilt the string every frame, allocating garbage. The count is now pushed only when it changes (`AlienCounter.SetCount`, using TMP's non-allocating `SetText`).
4. **Every alien rolled a random number to fire on every `FixedUpdate`.** That's N random checks per step, back-row aliens shooting through their own fleet, and a `static` cooldown that every alien shared. The fleet now fires one shot on a timer from the bottom alien of a column, sometimes aimed at the player's column.
5. **The fleet's direction change relied on `Invoke("startSidewaysMotion", …)`.** It used a string method name (not refactor-safe, not camelCase), took two passes over the alien list, and started the bounds at `0` instead of ±infinity. It's replaced with a fixed drop distance tracked in `dropRemaining` and a single combined move-and-measure loop. Hard-coded `±16` bounds are replaced with bounds taken from the camera.
6. **Leftover clutter and broken components.** The template had redundant `fleet.GetComponent<AlienFleet>()` calls on a field already typed `AlienFleet`, empty `Start`/`Update` methods (Unity still calls them), and commented-out dead code. Tuning values were public statics, so they didn't show in the Inspector; they're now `[SerializeField]`. The Spaceship also had **two** Rigidbodies (one in the prefab, one added in the scene) and a `MeshCollider` with no mesh. The `List<GameObject>` is now a typed `List<Alien>`.

## Additional features (four or more required)

- **Story.** The ronin is **Oni**, once Emperor Kagetsune's guard. Kagetsune betrayed him on the Night of a Thousand Bells, sealing his court, his army and Oni's village into an armor that makes him deathless. Every enemy is a soul from that armor sent out in a borrowed body, which is why they come back each night and remember. Oni's soul tore free, but it can't rest while the armor holds the people he led there, so every death pulls him back to the abandoned temple on the hill where he used to play her flute, and he wakes in its spirit pool. Each boss is a victim too: Benibana, the court sword-dancer whose smoke he used and whose people he sealed; Utsusemi, the monk who sheltered Oni, wound into clockwork; and Shirogane, Oni's sworn partner, who saved his soul at the cost of her own. Their intro, phase and defeat lines tell it. The story, a file for each character and a Hades-style reactive dialogue design are in [Story/](Story/README.md).

- **Reactive dialogue, the Hades way.** The game remembers every run (a save in `PlayerPrefs`: deaths and wins against each boss, what killed you last time, which lines you've heard) and picks what each character says from conversations written for situations. The first time you meet a boss you hear its full intro; die to Benibana and next time she opens with "Back so soon?"; after three deaths to her she counts them; beat her twice and she tells you which bell is hers, which Shirogane later answers. Story lines play once and in order, reactions rest a few runs before they repeat, and every boss keeps short repeatable lines drawn so nothing repeats until all have played. The boss who kills you has the last word, and as he climbs out of the temple's pool Oni says a few words after a death. F7 (dev tools) prints what each speaker could say and why the rest can't play. Lines not yet recorded show as subtitles. (`Dialogue.cs`, `SaveData.cs`, research in [Story/DIALOGUE_RESEARCH.md](Story/DIALOGUE_RESEARCH.md))
- **Talk scenes, like Hades.** Conversations play as scenes over the frozen field: the speaker's painting stands large at the left, Oni's at the right, and the line types into a dark plate under the speaker's name and title on a brush-ink tab; whoever isn't talking drops back into shadow, and Space, Enter or a click moves on. Every boss fight opens with one after the boss's title card and ends with one as the boss falls, and the first time you meet a boss, break its phase or beat it, Oni answers. A phase break gets a scene only when there's something new to say; otherwise its card speaks the line. The first time each soul (Akagane of Fire, Mizore of Frost, Hibiki of Storm) offers you its boons in a run, it talks to Oni before the cards appear: a chain of first meetings told one per run, reactions to how your last run ended and what you hold, and short greetings. The three souls were painted in the bosses' portrait style with GPT image generation. During play, lines show as plain subtitles with no portrait. (`DialogueScene`, `Dialogue.cs`)

- **Soul Embers and the Flute Songs (meta-progression).** Like Hades' Mirror of Night or Vampire Survivors' PowerUps, every kill earns Soul Embers that are kept when you die (1 a samurai, 5 a scout, 25 to 100 a boss). They're spent at the flute altar in the temple: small stat songs (a wider parry window, faster feet, swing and shuriken, longer invulnerability, a fourth life, and Second Dawn, which raises Oni once a run) and songs that give control over a run's rewards rather than power: Third Path (three portals after each round), Fate's Thread (up to three boon redraws a run, R), Banishing Bell (B takes a boon out of the run), Pond's Blessing (a second Spirit Bead after the first boss) and Ancestral Lore (each stage's first soul is Rare or better). A fresh save plays exactly like the tuned game, so the first run is beatable; the songs only add on top, about twice the survivability and no flat damage, the same size Hades and Vampire Survivors land on. Prices climb Vampire Survivors-style, with a refund and a switch to turn maxed songs off. (`Upgrades.cs`, design and research in [ECONOMY.md](ECONOMY.md))
- **The Oath Stone (Heat), after the first dawn.** In the temple's lower-left corner stands a stone bound in chains; once Kagetsune has fallen the chains break and, like Hades' Pact of Punishment, it takes oaths that make every run harder: faster armies, more and faster arrows, tougher warlords, the boss portal opening later, no Spirit Beads behind the portals, one life fewer. Each rank sworn is a point of Heat, which raises the embers earned by 10% a point, and the first dawn at a new highest Heat pays a bounty (150 embers per point). The Memorial stones show the highest Heat cleared. (`Oaths.cs`)

- **Three save slots and the temple between runs, like Hades.** START opens CHOOSE A JOURNEY: three slots, each showing its runs, embers, deaths and dawns, and ERASE A JOURNEY (with a confirm). An empty slot is a new game: the ronin puts his flute away, stands and walks into the first run, as Hades' first run starts straight in Tartarus. A journey already begun opens in the **temple**, the abandoned shrine on the hill, the House of Hades of this game. It's a PixelLab map with its gate at the top and a glowing spirit pool at the bottom, its stations built into the courtyard: the flute altar on a stone dais between two lit toro lanterns, the memorial stones in a kerbed grave nook behind their offerings, the weapon rack on a plank platform under a broken eave. Incense rises off the altar, light glints on the blades, lantern light flickers on the stones and ghost-fires flicker at the courtyard's edges. Oni walks behind and around the props and the stone lanterns, and takes the steps up to the altar's dais and the side step onto the rack's platform. The pool is alive: light plays on the water, rings spread on it and little beady-eyed souls drift up out of it and off the flagstones all around; when Oni steps in he wades, the water hiding his feet, rings following his steps, splashing in and out (`SoulPool`). It is also the only place Oni can walk in every direction (WASD or the arrows, in eight directions: his PixelLab run sideways and an eight-frame walk up, down and on each diagonal). There is no game-over screen: when Oni dies, the boss that killed him has its last word, the dark comes down and he wakes in the spirit pool like Zagreus rising from the Styx, climbs out as the soul-fire flares, sees what the run earned, and sometimes says something. Walk up to a station and press E: the **flute altar** (the Flute Songs, Hades' Mirror of Night), the **memorial stones** (runs, dawns and deaths, and each warlord he has met by how often they've killed each other; unmet ones stay worn smooth), the **weapon rack** (katana or kunai, Hades' Infernal Arms, kept in the save) and the **gate**, which sets out on the next run. The gate works: its two doors swing open with a creak as Oni walks up to it and shut behind him when he walks away, and setting out he walks through the doorway into the dark road beyond and under the gate's beam, which hides him as he goes. Victory and the pause menu's RETURN TO TEMPLE bring him back the same way. Safe areas are data (a map, a walkable floor, a wake spot and a list of stations), so more can be added later. (`SafeArea`, `SaveData.cs`, `GameManager.EnterSafeArea`)
- **Menus and start/end screens.** A title screen laid out like a Final Fantasy title: the Ronin's Revenge logo centred high on a paper-white page, a short left-aligned menu column under it (Start / Controls / Settings / Quit; Start opens the save slots) with the selection marked by a red diamond and red ink, and the ronin sitting on his row in the bottom-left corner playing his flute, little pixel music notes in his spirit teal drifting up from it (his idle stands in until the flute frames are drawn). Starting a new journey fades the logo and menu away; the ronin puts his flute away and gets to his feet (a PixelLab stand-up clip), then walks out to the middle of his row while the paper page fades into the Bamboo Road under him, and the first stage card paints on. Any key skips it. Pages opened from the title (Controls, Settings, Dev Tools) stay on its white page in ink; opened from the pause menu, they sit over the dimmed fight. Controls shows each action with its keys drawn as key caps plus the rules in three lines; Settings has Music and Sound volume sliders that apply instantly and are saved with `PlayerPrefs`. Esc pauses mid-fight (Resume / Return to Temple / Restart / Controls / Settings / Quit to Title). Before each round a stage card paints on across the middle of the field in the boss cards' style: a stroke of dry-brush ink, a small "Stage I · Round 1" overline, the stage's name in large Roman capitals, a brush rule with a diamond in the round's colour, and a line of text, each rising in turn, while the army marches in above and below it. The Victory screen shows score, high score and how far you got, calls out a new high score, and offers Return to Temple / Title; a death goes straight back to the temple. The selected menu row is marked by a red diamond, a slight scale-up and, over the dimmed fight, a stroke of red ink that paints on behind it, with sounds on move and confirm. High score is saved with `PlayerPrefs`. (`GameManager`, `MenuUI`, `GameUI`)
- **Readable UI.** Following the Xbox Accessibility Guidelines and Game Accessibility Guidelines: no text below 26px at 1080p, every piece of text outlined and drop-shadowed so it reads over the busy playfield, the decorative brush font kept to titles with a clean font for everything else, and no italics. The HUD keeps a minimal arcade layout (score in a single row top left; the stage number top right with the round on a smaller line beneath it, right-aligned; a small dim label beside each number; lives bottom left) on a soft shade, leaving the top centre free for a compact boss bar that sits above the bosses rather than over them. Boss lines show on a dark plate with the speaker's name. (`GameUI.Style`, `TextStyle`)
- **Additional levels: stages of rounds.** The game is built from stages (`StageConfig`), each a run of rounds (`LevelConfig`) followed by a boss, configured in `GameManager.stages`. Stage 1, **The Bamboo Road**, has three configured rounds and then Benibana. Each round brings a bigger army (8×3, then 9×4, then 10×4) that starts lower, marches faster, and shoots more often with more aimed, faster arrows. Each new army marches down into the field from above the screen to the drum while the stage card shows, and it can't be hit until the round starts. The blockers are rebuilt only at the start of a stage, so the damage they take carries through all its rounds. Beating a stage earns a boon and a Spirit Bead, and the HUD shows the stage and round.
- **Portals between rounds, like Hades' chamber doors.** Each round is fought for a reward, named on its banner and given when the army falls: a stage's first round always ends in a soul. Then two portals open where the army stood, each showing what the next round will give (a Fire, Frost or Storm soul, a Spirit Bead, an ember cache, a healing spring while hurt, or a technique scroll), and the ronin walks his row and steps up into the one he wants. From round 3 the warlord's portal opens beside them, but you can keep going: a stage runs up to 5 rounds, the extra ones repeating its last army faster, with more arrows, a commander rank and, in round 5, one more rank. Going deeper builds a stronger ronin at the cost of more risk before the boss. Each portal shows its reward as animated pixel art (PixelLab): the souls as their paintings on the boon screen show them, each flame wisp over its bell (Akagane's bronze bell, Mizore's silver one, Hibiki's red cord of tin bells), a glowing prayer bead, a dish of smouldering embers, a bubbling pink spring, a tied steel-blue scroll, and for the warlord's portal something of theirs: Benibana's kiseru with her rosary wound round it, smoke curling up; Utsusemi's great hat with its slit-eye glowing beneath; the head of Kuon, Shirogane's spirit wolf; and Kagetsune's horned helm hung with bells. (`Portal.cs`, `GameManager.ChoosePortal`)
- **Samurai kinds.** Rows take their kind from `LevelConfig.rowKinds` (`SamuraiKind`). **Ashigaru** foot soldiers loose single arrows. **Archers** loose a fan of three arrows and are worth 30 points. **Taishō** commanders are the top tier: they take two hits, are worth 60 points, and every 3 to 6 seconds one of them hurls a **heavy spear** over its own ranks. The spear can't be destroyed: slash waves and arrows break on it and it cuts through cover, so the only answers are to dodge it or parry it. A parried spear flies back up as a riposte that runs through three samurai. Archers first appear in the top row in round 2; in round 3 the Taishō hold the top row with archers below them. (`Samurai`, `SamuraiArmy`, `Arrow.MakeUnbreakable`)
- **Kite scouts (the mystery ship).** In round 3 a kite-borne scout crosses the top of the screen every 15 to 25 seconds. One hit brings it down for 100, 150 or 300 bonus points (multiplied by the stage number) and a guaranteed soul. (`Scout`)
- **Stage 2: The Pilgrim's Pass.** A blue-clan army. Round 1 adds a front rank of **Shieldbearers** (steel armour, two hits, never fire) that the archers shoot over. Round 2 adds **divers**: every few seconds a samurai from behind the front rank flashes, cries out and swoops down at the ronin, smashing through cover, before gliding back into formation. Round 3 mixes everything with scouts. (`SamuraiKind.Shieldbearer`, `LevelConfig.diveInterval`, `SamuraiArmy`)
- **Stage 3: The Frozen Shrine.** A pale, frostbitten army on a snowbound shrine path, and the hardest rounds of the run: 11×5, 12×5 and then 12×6 samurai starting lower and marching faster, with divers from round 1, scouts from round 2, and two Taishō ranks in round 3. The new kind is the **Yurei**: ghosts of fallen samurai, drawn see-through in cyan over a spirit glow that breathes in and out. A yurei looses a parted pair of **spirit arrows that drift straight through cover**, so the blockers only shelter you from the living, and it's worth 35 points. (`SamuraiKind.Yurei`, `Samurai.Fire`, `GameManager.DefaultStages`; the stages are copied into the scene by `Ninja/Apply Stages`)
- **Stage 4: The Mountain Shrine.** The stair up to the hall where the Night of a Thousand Bells was held, under a starless sky, and the last and hardest rounds: 12×5, 12×6 and then 13×6 samurai in crimson and bronze, every kind at once (Taishō, archers, yurei, shieldbearers) with divers throughout and scouts from round 2. The new kind is the **Bellringer**, an Imperial shrine guard with a hand bell: it never shoots and takes two hits, but every few seconds one of them glows and **tolls, and the whole army fires twice as fast** for a moment. Cut one down before its bell rings and the toll never comes; every bell you silence chimes softly and delays the next toll. It's worth 50 points. (`SamuraiKind.Bellringer`, `SamuraiArmy.UpdateBells`, `Samurai.WarnToll`/`Toll`)
- **Boss: Benibana, the Crimson Smoke.** After Stage 1's last round, the music cuts out, smoke rolls across the top of the screen, a blood moon turns the courtyard red, and Benibana descends. She's a red-haired ronin in a torn black kimono who fights with a kiseru pipe, a rosary chain and a katana. Her entrance is a cinematic in the style of a Final Fantasy XIV boss card: on a dark stage lit crimson, her full-body portrait fades up and drifts closer, then cross-fades to her close-up while a lower-third title rises in (her epithet, a thin rule drawing out from the centre, and her name, in Roman capitals), and a talk scene follows where she speaks her voiced line. She has two 70 HP bars, shown on a boss bar; the second fills in after her phase break. She blinks around the top of the screen and chains moves into combos with short recoveries:
  - **Kiseru Smoke Rings:** volleys of slow rings that swell as they fall, with each volley filling the gaps left by the last.
  - **Rosary Lash:** a wide red band marks a column and narrows to a line over about a second, then her chain slams down it, smashing blockers. It hangs there as a wall that can't be parried, and then its crosses break off and fall.
  - **Crimson Iaido:** a red line flashes, then she draws and a crescent slash sweeps down at the ronin, followed by a second draw aimed where he dodged. The crescent can only be parried: slashes, the shuriken and cover don't stop it. **Parry it on time and it flies back at her**, shattering any Iaido crescent in its path. It deals no damage itself, but it breaks her stance and leaves her open to double damage.
  - **Phase 2 (first bar emptied):** in a short cutscene, invincible while her HP bar refills, she staggers, draws on her pipe as the smoke rushes back into her and a crimson glow builds, the music falls away for a breath, and then her blade comes out in a crossing crimson cut that tears her kimono and blows the smoke off her: she stands revealed in her new form (sprite and all), and a second title card plays, **The Crimson Unsealed**: her new full-body painting (hair loose, tattoos burning, katana drawn) cross-fades to a close-up while she speaks a furious line. She exhales a smoke veil over the top of the screen and fights from inside it, with only her glowing eyes showing. Fire boons burn holes in the veil. She's wreathed in crimson flames and fights faster, with three-move combos, extra chains, an X-shaped Iaido, crosses hidden in her smoke rings, and the **Rosary Bloom**: three rotating waves of crosses across the whole field.
  - Boon effects work on her too: fire bursts, frost freezes her (and a slash on her ice shatters it for extra damage), and lightning arcs into her. When she falls she kneels and fades, the blood moon lifts, and her soul becomes the stage's boon. (`Boss`, `Benibana`, `GameUI.PlayBossIntro`; her pixel art was made with PixelLab and is wired up by `Ninja/Apply Boss Art`)
- **Lives.** 3 lives, shown as small pixel hearts in a strip along the very bottom-left, below the ronin's feet so they never hide him, with an empty outlined heart for each one lost. The Water boon Tidal Renewal is the only way to raise the maximum (up to 5). A blocker worn down by hits has a 20% chance to drop a heart that heals one lost life; at full health the ronin can't take it, so it waits on his row until he's been hurt, and at the end of a round any heart he can use flies to him. After a hit the ronin plays his hit animation, blinks and is invulnerable for 2 s. (`Ronin`, `GameUI.SetLives`)
- **Boss: Utsusemi, the Hollow Pilgrim.** Stage 2 ends under a pale steel moon with a karakuri automaton: a clockwork shell wearing a dead pilgrim's wide hat and lace shroud, with a red slit-eye and a lantern-red heart. It has two 120 HP bars and doesn't blink like Benibana; it stalks the top of the screen in stiff, ticking steps that leave flickering steel-blue and gold digital afterimages, and every attack keeps time to its clock. Its entrance is a show of force: as soon as it descends, before its title card, its slit-eye charges and a thin red laser rakes the whole row of blockers, bursting every one and leaving a line of fire burning where they stood, so this duel has no courtyard cover:
  - **Kasa Wheel:** a dotted gold trail shows the loop, then it hurls its hat like a boomerang: it swoops down into your lane where you stand, skims along the ground and curls back up to its head, shedding gold tassel weights. Dodge out of the lane or parry it; a parried hat flies back and circles it as your riposte, and only reappears on its head once that riposte fades. The fight carries on while the hat is out, and with its eye exposed **every hit deals double damage**.
  - Its own shots pass through one another, so a swinging weight or a flying hat is never cut down by its lace, tassels or cleave; only your attacks break them.
  - **Lace Lattice:** a curtain of lace diamonds with a gap or two drifts down through the cover. Each diamond breaks in one hit, so you can cut your own way through; fire boons burn holes in it.
  - **Hour Strikes:** it announces the move ("Remember the hours... one... by one.") with a shimmering gold outline: **super armour**: while it speaks, hits do only 5% of their damage and nothing staggers or freezes it. It lasers the hours one at a time (three; five in phase 2), **where you stand**: a thin red sight line from its eye follows you, then locks on and brightens, and a moment later its eye fires a laser onto the spot, so step off as soon as it locks. The armour drops and it counts the hours again with its blade: for each one the sight line **hunts you down and locks on**, an hour's numeral (I, II, III…, a brass Roman numeral on a brass ring, glowing red-hot, made in PixelLab) rises where you stood, and a moment later it dashes down out of its row onto that spot, chops it with a ground-cracking blow (the numeral flares red) and dashes back up, so step off as soon as it locks. Then, back in its row, it strikes **the hour**: the next numeral (IV, or VI in phase 2) rises under you and follows you, its core glows as it draws its blade back over three ticks, and its swing flings a giant crescent cleave at you. It hunts you down, breaks your shots and cuts through cover, so **only a parry answers it**. Parry it and its gears jam for a long stagger. While it's down in your lane between chops, it's in easy reach of your shots.
  - **Plumb Lines:** gold clockwork portals open one by one just above its head, behind it. Their threads show their full reach, then a gold weight drops from each and swings as a pendulum through your lane, tilting with its thread, the swings rippling along the row like a pendulum-wave toy, with one right above you. A hit cuts a weight loose: its thread parts with a spray of gold and a high ting, the loose end tumbles away, and the rest whips back up wriggling into its portal, which flares and closes; when time is up the swings die down, the rest are reeled back up into their portals, and the portals close.
  - **Phase 2 (first bar emptied):** in a short cutscene, invincible while its HP bar refills, its mainspring slips and it **overloads**: it shudders in place as glowing cracks spread from its core over its body and hat, stage by stage, its clock ticking faster and faster, while its eye and core loose lasers wildly in every direction, red at first and then molten orange as the cracks open, ever faster (they scorch the ground but can't hurt you). It stops dead as a bell tolls once, then breaks open, gears and parts bursting off it, its hat shattering into a halo and its core blazing out in a burst of light; it hangs there in its new form (sprite and all), and a second title card plays, **The Unwound Hour**: its new full-body painting (hat split into a broken halo, shroud torn, core blazing) cross-fades to a close-up as it speaks: from here it fights mostly with lasers, one at least every other move. Its lasers turn **molten orange** to match its blazing core: a wilder beam that thrashes in width and spits embers along its length (phase 1's beam is a steadier crimson; both are PixelLab beam art tiled and scrolled along the laser). Its blade glows molten too, in hand, wheeling and planted. The clock speeds up: three-move combos, two lattice layers and five hours on a faster beat. Its Kasa Wheel becomes the **Shattered Halo**: the broken halo bursts apart off its head into three pieces (PixelLab art matched to the hat). The crown flies the hat's old loop at you; a beat later the two side shards cross over its head, dive in either side of you and skim away outward, so there's room just beside where you stood but not far along the lane. Each piece has its own trail of dots and is parried on its own, and every hit is doubled until all three are home. Then its sword wheels end over end around a loop of its own, aimed at wherever you dodged (parryable like the hat); while it's out it fights on empty-handed with only its lace and plumbs.
    - **Heart Lantern:** first it **throws its sword at you**: an hour's numeral rises where it will land, like the Hour Strikes dashes, the sword wheels end over end and plants itself point-down there (it cuts you if you're still standing on the numeral). The planted sword, shimmering with a ward, is your only cover, and a **safe-zone marker** shows exactly where it shelters you: a cool cyan gate of light on the ground with a shield icon bobbing over it (PixelLab art, reusable for any boss), centred on the sword: anywhere inside its ward is safe, plus the shade it casts beyond, worked out from the laser's own rays. It brightens while the laser fires. Sparks pour into the heart as it whines up, then a three-layer laser from the heart sweeps the ground, flashes a warning and whips back in an instant; shelter on the far side of the sword from the boss. The beam only strikes sparks off the sword, leaving a trail of fire burning along the ground. When the laser ends the blade glints, and it dashes down to the sword and wrenches it out with a strike, so get clear of the sword before it arrives.
    - **Midnight:** it throws its sword at you (dodge it), and two clock-hand lasers, one from its slit-eye and one from its core, sweep in from the screen's edges, closing on the planted sword from both sides. Beside the sword is the only safe ground, marked by the same safe-zone gate. As they meet, a bell strikes and they flash, then snap back out. - **New abilities.** The **Spirit Shuriken** is a big spinning blade that cuts through a whole column (6 s cooldown). **Parry** is a quick katana stance. A parry that deflects something is ready again at once, so well-timed parries chain; a missed parry (one that deflects nothing) has a 2 s cooldown. A parried arrow flies back as itself, wrapped in a pulsing parry aura with a trail of afterimages, and homes in on a random enemy still standing (a samurai, the scout or the boss), passing the other enemies and arrows on the way (but not your own cover, which stops it) and picking a new target if its own falls first. Both projectiles go through `Kunai`, which also handles piercing.
- **Bullet-hell rules, taught boss by boss.** You only move left and right, so every falling enemy shot (arrows included) marks the spot where it will land on your row, a glow in its own colour that brightens as it nears (`ArrowShadow`). Patterns are built so that wherever they cross your row they leave a gap nearly twice your width, and the next gap is never further than you can comfortably run before it lands (`BulletHell`). Three kinds of shot recur: ordinary shots you can parry; **crimson** shots no parry turns (the first time a parry fizzles on one, the game tells you to dodge the crimson); and **bronze** shots that, parried, ring out a **Resonance** that bursts the breakable shots around them, opening a hole in a wall. Earlier bosses teach two of them: Benibana's Rosary Bloom ends with a single curtain of crosses with one wide gap, and after Shirogane's Phantom Blade Rain a short fence of ghost swords falls over you with a bronze ringing blade at its heart (walk round it, or parry the blade). Against the Emperor's curtains a parry sends back at most three skulls and cuts the rest down, so parrying into a stream opens a way without filling the field with ripostes. His bullets sit on their own physics layer (`BossBullet`) so a screen full of them stays cheap.
- **Katana slash instead of kunai.** The main attack is a katana swing that sends a crescent slash wave up the screen; it plays exactly like the old kunai throw (same cooldown, damage and arrow-cancelling). The kunai, its sprites and its old boon icons are kept (`Art/Sprites/UI/Weapons/Kunai/`) and selectable through `Ronin.weapon`, ready for a weapon-select screen.
- **New background.** Stage 1, the Bamboo Road, is a strict top-down dirt road with cart ruts and fallen bamboo leaves, with the bamboo grove kept to thin strips at the very edges so nothing tall breaks the top-down view of the marching army (`Backdrop`). The original moonlit grass courtyard is kept for a later stage. It's top-down to match the 3/4-view character sprites, so the armies stand on the ground instead of floating in front of a sky.
- **Atmosphere.** Every map has its own mood laid over it, built in code (`Atmosphere`): on the Bamboo Road big dappled cloud shadows slide down the road and darken everything they pass over, soldiers, the ronin and cover included, moonlight falls from the moon's corner and the far side sinks into shadow, and fireflies drift over the field; the Pilgrim's Pass has quicker clouds and low mist rolling over the stones; Benibana's shrine glows red under her blood moon with embers in the air; Utsusemi's temple has still air, dust hanging in a cold shaft of moonlight and a creeping floor mist; the Frozen Shrine path has heavy slow snow clouds, mist in the hollows and snowflakes in the air, and Shirogane's court is dead still, spirit light pooling on the frost under a thick cyan mist and drifting spirit motes. Hit sparks, slashes and boss effects stay bright over the shadows, and the mist and moonlight lie under the characters, so the fight stays readable. The Mountain Shrine and the Emperor's courtyard have no moonlight at all: heavy dark at the edges, crimson smoke low on the ground and teal souls drifting in the air, until the dawn warms it all at the end.
- **Maps and travel between them.** Each boss has its own arena and each stage has its own ground, all generated with PixelLab in the courtyard's style: Benibana waits in a shrine court strewn with crimson maple leaves, Stage 2 is fought on the weathered stone and gravel of the Pilgrim's Pass, Utsusemi waits on a steel-blue temple floor inlaid with brass clockwork, Stage 3 climbs a snowbound shrine path between stone lanterns and snow-laden pines, and Shirogane waits in a frozen shrine court glazed with ice, a ritual ring of frost-rimmed tiles glowing cyan at its middle, under a spirit-blue moon that turns the snow to drifting light. ## Balancing

The template was easy because the player could fire 20 shots per second and the fleet never sped up. Changes:

- Player fire cooldown raised from 0.05 s to 0.35 s.
- The army speeds up as it shrinks: up to 3.5× in round 1, rising to 4.2× by round 3.
- The army grows each round (24 / 36 / 40 samurai) and starts closer to the player, archers add three-arrow fans from round 2, and Taishō add unbreakable spears in round 3. Stage 2 adds Shieldbearers and divers; Stage 3 (55 / 60 / 72 samurai) adds Yurei whose arrows ignore cover, divers from its first round and a second Taishō rank. Stage 4 (60 / 72 / 78 samurai) mixes every kind with Bellringers, divers throughout and scouts from round 2.
- Arrows come from the front line, partly aimed at the player, and get faster each round.
- The blockers aren't rebuilt between rounds, so cover wears down as the stage goes on.
- Benibana is meant to be the hardest part of Stage 1: dense, combo-chained patterns, an unparryable chain, and a faster second phase. Her opening comes from parrying the Iaido back at her. Shirogane is the hardest fight before the end: two 140 HP bars, lanes and fans that always leave a way through but keep moving, ice pillars to parry or dodge and glacier spires that must be dodged, a parry-only draw in each phase, and from her second phase Kuon, an 80 HP partner on the same bar that hunts on its own clock. Whichever of them falls first, the other fights on enraged. Her openings are a parried Moonsplitter, the grief stagger when Kuon falls, and the two seconds she (or enraged Kuon) stands spent after each ultimate. Kagetsune is the final exam, tuned to beat a full set of boons: two 210 HP bars, short recoveries and long combos, bullet-hell curtains that always leave a gap you can reach, a seal that takes away your parry, and in phase 2 the other three bosses' moves layered over his own. His openings are the parried Last Toll and Soul Coffin, the parried soul blows, and the moment he kneels after the Night of a Thousand Bells; an opening doubles damage, but not on top of a stagger's own double.
- The game is still beatable thanks to 3 lives, invulnerability after a hit, and the courtyard blockers.
- The Flute Songs never weaken a fresh start: all of the above is the tuning a first run gets. Bought songs add at most a life, a revive, about 10-15% quicker tools and more control over the rewards, so later runs get easier without the first one being made harder ([ECONOMY.md](ECONOMY.md)).
- Because the base game is hard, souls give the player a way to build up power over a run. The portals, scouts and the end of each stage all give boons, and the Spirit Shuriken and Parry give tools for clearing columns and dealing with arrows. The rounds per stage, when the boss portal opens, how many portals open, and the extra rounds' speed-up are tunable on `GameManager`.

All of these values can be tuned in the Inspector on `GameManager` (the `stages` array, with each stage's `rounds`), `SamuraiArmy`, `Ronin` and `Blockers`. Benibana's timings are in `Benibana.cs`, Utsusemi's in `Utsusemi.cs`, Shirogane's in `Shirogane.cs`, Kagetsune's in `Kagetsune.cs`.

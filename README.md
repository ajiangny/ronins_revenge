# Ronin's Revenge — Assignment 1

A Space Invaders remake re-themed as a lone chibi ronin holding a moonlit temple courtyard against marching samurai armies.

Built on the class template ([Oren-Steinberg/Space-Invaders-3D](https://github.com/Oren-Steinberg/Space-Invaders-3D)), Unity 6000.6.6f1, URP, the new Input System.

**Running it:** open the project in Unity 6000.6.6f1, open `Assets/Scenes/SampleScene.unity` and press Play, or build the `Web` build profile (Build Profiles → Web → Build) and serve the output folder; the web build also runs on itch.io. Fights are played on keyboard or keyboard and mouse; a controller only works in the menus and the temple.

The template's Space Invaders classes, prefabs and layers are renamed to the theme: `Alien` → `Samurai`, `AlienFleet` → `SamuraiArmy`, `AlienBullet` → `Arrow`, `AlienCounter` → `SamuraiCounter`, `Spaceship` → `Ronin`, `Dart` → `Projectile`, `ShieldWall`/`ShieldBlock` → `BlockerWall`/`Blocker`. The section on template code issues below uses the template's original names.

## Controls

Two schemes, picked on two cards (KEYBOARD / KEYBOARD + MOUSE, each listing its keys as drawn caps, with mouse buttons and the spacebar as icons) when a new journey starts, changeable under SETTINGS or on the CONTROLS page (PLAY WITH), and switched automatically mid-fight when you use the other one's buttons (a mouse click, or a Keyboard-only attack key); every key prompt (Tab tips, Poguru's lessons, the Controls and Tips pages) shows the scheme in use, and a lesson's key caps redraw if you switch mid-lesson.

- **Keyboard:** one hand moves, the other attacks, in either of two layouts that work together. Arcade: the arrows move, Space slashes (hold), C parries, X throws the Spirit Shuriken, Z unleashes the ultimate. Or A/D move with J slash, K parry, L shuriken, I ultimate.
- **Keyboard + Mouse:** A/D move, hold Left Click to slash, Right Click to parry, Space for the Spirit Shuriken, E (or Middle Click) for the ultimate.
- **Temple:** WASD or the arrows walk anywhere; E uses a station.
- **Pause:** Esc or P (in a browser's fullscreen, where Esc may only leave fullscreen, P pauses, and leaving fullscreen mid-fight pauses too).
- **Menus:** Up/Down (or W/S) to select, Left/Right to adjust a slider, Enter or Space to confirm, Esc to go back; the mouse and a gamepad work too.
- **Boon screen:** Up/Down (or W/S, or 1/2/3) to select, Enter or click to choose, R to redraw and B to banish once the Flute Songs for them are learned, and X twice (or a click on the hint) to reject every offer and take nothing.
- **Between rounds:** walk under a path (a tooltip under it says what it gives) and press E or Enter to choose it. Enter or Space skips a boss's intro.
- **Dev tools** (Editor and development builds, and release builds while the GameManager's "Dev Tools In Release" box is ticked, as it is in the submitted build so graders can reach every stage and boss): DEV TOOLS on the title and pause menus (or F3) starts any stage or boss fight directly and toggles god mode (also F4); F1 clears the current army, F2 jumps to the last boss, F5 drops a soul (Fire, Frost, Storm, Water and Steel in turn; a Water drop also marks Asagi as met, a Steel drop Oliver), F6 drops a technique scroll, F7 prints what each speaker could say and why, F8 prints the balancing run log (also written to `runlog.txt` in the game's data folder), F9 charges the ultimate, F10 opens a Spirit Bead and F11 a Spirit Thread; the DEV TOOLS menu also has MEET EVERY FOE and FORGET EVERY FOE for the memorial stones' encyclopedia.

## Code issues found in the template and fixed

1. **Dynamic Rigidbodies were moved by writing `transform.position` every physics step.** This fights the physics engine and teleports colliders. Aliens and the ship are now kinematic and move with `Rigidbody.MovePosition` (interpolated). Darts and alien bullets are launched once with `linearVelocity`.
2. **Projectiles destroyed whatever they hit (`Destroy(coll.gameObject)`).** An alien bullet would delete the ship, a shield, or anything else outright, which made lives and shields impossible to add. Now each object handles its own collision: `Alien` dies, `Spaceship` loses a life, `ShieldBlock` takes damage, and the projectile only destroys itself. `Dart.TryConsume()` keeps one dart from killing two aliens in the same step.
3. **The UI was polled and rebuilt every frame.** `AlienFleet.Update` copied the count into `AlienCounter`, and `AlienCounter.Update` rebuilt the string every frame, allocating garbage. The count is now pushed only when it changes (`AlienCounter.SetCount`, using TMP's non-allocating `SetText`).
4. **Every alien rolled a random number to fire on every `FixedUpdate`.** That's N random checks per step, back-row aliens shooting through their own fleet, and a `static` cooldown that every alien shared. The fleet now fires one shot on a timer from the bottom alien of a column, sometimes aimed at the player's column.
5. **The fleet's direction change relied on `Invoke("startSidewaysMotion", …)`.** It used a string method name (not refactor-safe, not camelCase), took two passes over the alien list, and started the bounds at `0` instead of ±infinity. It's replaced with a fixed drop distance tracked in `dropRemaining` and a single combined move-and-measure loop. Hard-coded `±16` bounds are replaced with bounds taken from the camera.
6. **Leftover clutter and broken components.** The template had redundant `fleet.GetComponent<AlienFleet>()` calls on a field already typed `AlienFleet`, empty `Start`/`Update` methods (Unity still calls them), and commented-out dead code. Tuning values were public statics, so they didn't show in the Inspector; they're now `[SerializeField]`. The Spaceship also had **two** Rigidbodies (one in the prefab, one added in the scene) and a `MeshCollider` with no mesh. The `List<GameObject>` is now a typed `List<Alien>`.

### Story & dialogue

- **Story.** The ronin is **Oni**, once Emperor Kagetsune's guard. Kagetsune betrayed him on the Night of a Thousand Bells, sealing his court, his army and Oni's village into an armor that makes him deathless. Every enemy is a soul from that armor sent out in a borrowed body, which is why they come back each night and remember. Oni's soul tore free, but it can't rest while the armor holds the people he led there, so every death pulls him back to the abandoned temple on the hill where he used to play her flute, and he wakes in its spirit pool. Each boss is a victim too: Benibana, the court sword-dancer whose smoke he used and whose people he sealed; Utsusemi, the monk who sheltered Oni, wound into clockwork; and Shirogane, Oni's sworn partner, who saved his soul at the cost of her own. Their intro, phase and defeat lines tell it. The story, a file for each character and a Hades-style reactive dialogue design are in [Story/](Story/README.md).
- **Reactive dialogue, the Hades way.** The game remembers every run (a save in `PlayerPrefs`: deaths and wins against each boss, what killed you last time, which lines you've heard) and picks what each character says from conversations written for situations. The first time you meet a boss you hear its full intro; die to Benibana and next time she opens with "Back so soon?"; after three deaths to her she counts them; beat her twice and she tells you which bell is hers, which Shirogane later answers. Story lines play once and in order, reactions rest a few runs before they repeat, and every boss keeps short repeatable lines drawn so nothing repeats until all have played. The boss who kills you has the last word, and as he climbs out of the temple's pool Oni says a few words after a death, or the soul he leaned on most that run does. Poguru then roasts whatever killed you, down to what struck the last blow: an arrow ("The thing you cut in half for FUN?"), a Taisho's spear, a ghost arrow through cover, a diving soldier, the army marching all the way down, or each boss, and for a boss which attack it was (every boss move tags the shots it fires and the blows it lands, so the "hide behind a rock" roast only plays after Utsusemi's Hyper Beam, and the sword lasers, Benibana's iaido, Kuon's Maw and the Spirit Hyper Beam each have their own), with escalating lines for dying the same way again; now and then Oni snaps back or just grunts. At his nest Poguru does an impression of Oni's flat "...No." when Oni turns him down. The bosses react to which souls you carry (Kagetsune sneers at the village drum), and the souls meet Poguru and talk over each other. The cast also remember what you've found and whom you've met: the first time you get a Spirit Bead, Thread, technique scroll, Healing Spring, Spirit Notes, a Warlord's Seal or Kite Silk, or meet a new kind of soldier (archers, the Taisho, shieldbearers, divers, yurei, bellringers), Poguru pops out with a quick word on what it does, and at his nest he says more about it while it's still news, ahead of everything else (and about the Flute Song you just learned, the oath stone, the kunai, or meeting Asagi). Later the souls and bosses bring it up: Akagane on the warlords' seals, Shirogane on the guard's scrolls, Kagetsune once you've met all three souls. Major events like these jump the queue over the story chains. The bosses also talk about each other, since every fallen soul goes back into the same coffin: Benibana hears that you reached the clock, the Emperor broke, a bell went quiet; they keep score at milestones and Oni counts back because he is forgetting; Kagetsune notices Poguru hiding in the wisp, and everyone but the souls notices Kuon. Beat a boss again after she has told you how to free her and her defeat is a farewell; a freed soul stops answering when Kagetsune summons it in his second phase. F7 (dev tools) prints what each speaker could say and why the rest can't play. Lines not yet recorded show as subtitles. (`Dialogue.cs`, `SaveData.cs`, research in [Story/DIALOGUE_RESEARCH.md](Story/DIALOGUE_RESEARCH.md))
- **Talk scenes, like Hades.** Conversations play as scenes over the frozen field: the speaker's painting stands large at the left, Oni's at the right, and the line types into a dark plate under the speaker's name and title on a brush-ink tab; whoever isn't talking drops back into shadow, and Space, Enter or a click moves on. Every boss fight opens with one after the boss's title card and ends with one as the boss falls, and the first time you meet a boss, break its phase or beat it, Oni answers. A phase break's card speaks a line that follows the boss's arc and how the first phase went (untouched, on your last life, killed by this form last time); a scene plays only when Oni answers. The first time each soul (Akagane of Fire, Mizore of Frost, Hibiki of Storm) offers you its boons in a run, it talks to Oni before the cards appear: a chain of first meetings told one per run, reactions to how your last run ended and what you hold, and short greetings. As in Hades, each soul announces itself with its own sting: Akagane a war-drum boom and roaring flame, Mizore ice chimes over a singing bowl, Hibiki a taiko drumroll into thunder. A Legendary on offer gets its own lines, and a Duo plays both souls bickering; they mark the boss just felled or the one ahead, and sulk when another soul's element leads, with the rival answering back. Every line is voiced. The three souls were painted in the bosses' portrait style with GPT image generation. During play, lines show as plain subtitles with no portrait. (`DialogueScene`, `Dialogue.cs`)

### Run structure & levels

- **Additional levels: stages of rounds.** The game is built from stages (`StageConfig`), each a run of rounds (`LevelConfig`) followed by a boss, configured in `GameManager.stages`. Stage 1, **The Bamboo Road**, has three configured rounds and then Benibana. Each round brings a bigger army (8×3, then 9×4, then 10×4) that starts lower, marches faster, and shoots more often with more aimed, faster arrows. Each new army marches down into the field from above the screen to the drum while the stage card shows, and it can't be hit until the round starts. Every round is fought further along the road, so each one brings fresh cover. Beating a stage earns a boon and a Spirit Bead, and the HUD shows the stage and round.
- **Time bonus.** A clock under the score times each round and boss fight (only the fighting: banners, boon screens and talk scenes don't count, and the clock stops with the world in Utsusemi's Stopped Hour). Clearing a round under 40 seconds, or a whole stage under its par (40 seconds a round fought plus 2 minutes for its boss), banks points for every second to spare, scaled by the stage number; the clock flashes the time and the bonus in gold as each is cleared and dims once par has passed. The banked bonus is added to the score when the run ends, a death included, and the dawn results show the run's time and the bonus from rounds and stages. (`GameManager.Clock`, `GameUI.SetClock`)
- **Paths between rounds, like Hades' chamber doors.** Each round is fought for a reward shown on its banner: a soul, a Spirit Bead, a Spirit Thread, Spirit Notes, a healing spring or a technique scroll. When the army falls, two paths open (three with the Third Path song), each showing the next round's reward. From round 3 the warlord's path opens beside them, but a stage can run to 5 rounds, trading more risk for a stronger ronin before the boss.

### Enemies

- **Samurai kinds: an army of the dead.** The army is the dead of the Night of a Thousand Bells, sent back out each night in the bodies they died in: rotted armour and bone, a faint teal ember of soul in each eye, crumbling to bones and ash when cut down ([Story/Army.md](Story/Army.md)). Rows take their kind from `LevelConfig.rowKinds` (`SamuraiKind`). **Ashigaru**, skeletal foot soldiers in rotted straw hats, loose single arrows. **Archers**, corpse bushi in cracked lamellar, loose a fan of three arrows and are worth 30 points. **Taishō**, revenant generals in horned kabuto with cracked menpō over bare skulls, are the top tier: they take two hits, are worth 60 points, and every 3 to 6 seconds one of them hurls a **heavy spear** over its own ranks. The spear can't be destroyed: slash waves and arrows break on it and it cuts through cover, so the only answers are to dodge it or parry it. A parried spear flies back up as a riposte that runs through three samurai. Archers first appear in the top row in round 2; in round 3 the Taishō hold the top row with archers below them. (`Samurai`, `SamuraiArmy`, `Arrow.MakeUnbreakable`)
- **Formations, veterans and the Elite.** From round 3 of Stage 1 the army stops marching in a plain grid: its ranks can be **staggered** (every other rank offset half a step, so the gaps sit over the soldiers behind), split into **squads** with open lanes between them, and the layout carries on through every stage. Some ranks are **veterans**, steel-edged soldiers worth ten extra points who take two cuts (from Stage 2). Every round has one **Elite**, the Sworn Guard: a soldier of any kind, taller than the rest, edged and haloed in his clan's colour, who takes four hits (five in Stage 3, six in Stage 4, and one more again in each extra round), shoots on his own timer on top of the army's fire, is worth triple points, and always lets a soul slip free, even as the last soldier standing. (`SamuraiArmy.Formation.cs`/`Elite.cs`, `Samurai.Rank.cs`, `LevelConfig.shape`/`rowHealth`/`eliteHealth`)
- **Volleys and three kinds of arrow.** From Stage 2 the army shoots in **volleys**: two or three shooters from different columns at once (always at least two columns apart, so there is a gap to stand in), with a slower beat between volleys. Some arrows fly **fast**, some are **shielded** (a white ring round them: it takes two cuts or one shuriken to break), and from Stage 3 some are **guarded** (red): blades break on them, so they must be parried or dodged, though the cover still stops them. (`SamuraiArmy.FireShot`/`RollMods`, `Arrow.Guard`)
- **Kite scouts (the mystery ship).** In round 3 a kite-borne scout, a shrouded corpse lashed to a torn paper kite, drifts across the top of the screen every 15 to 25 seconds. One hit brings it down for 100, 150 or 300 bonus points (multiplied by the stage number) and a guaranteed soul. (`Scout`)
- **A last stand at the cover, and parrying bodies.** When the army reaches the cover it doesn't end the run: each samurai that reaches the cover's row breaks formation and walks it on its own, left or right, turning at the edges and passing through one another, smashing the cover as it goes, while the ranks behind keep marching down and collapsing onto it. Every few seconds a samurai nearest the ronin shakes under a red **!** and lunges straight at him, always at the same steady speed. Divers wear the same **!** before they swoop, and every warning also draws a red streak from the samurai down to the ronin, a red mark at his feet, a sharp ring and a small jolt, so it is seen even from the corner of the eye. Only one comes at a time, and once the army holds the cover only lunges do (a diver from there would come out of nowhere). A diver swoops down past the ronin's row and glides back up to its place in plain sight, never off the screen. A lunge or dive that reaches him costs a life and the samurai walks back to its slot, but parrying it flings the samurai away in the parry's colour to die (and readies the parry again, like a parried arrow). (`SamuraiArmy.StartLunge`/`UpdateLunge`/`DiveContact`, `Samurai.WarnDive`/`Knock`, `Ronin.ParryBody`)
- **Bullet-hell rules, taught boss by boss.** You only move left and right, so every falling enemy shot (arrows included) marks the spot where it will land on your row, a glow in its own colour that brightens as it nears (`ArrowShadow`). Patterns are built so that wherever they cross your row they leave a gap nearly twice your width, and the next gap is never further than you can comfortably run before it lands (`BulletHell`). One colour language covers every shot, the army's and the warlords' (`ShotClass`): a **white** glow means a shot you can cut or parry; **red** means parry-only (blades break on it, and a parry gives a heavy riposte); **violet** means dodge-only, no parry turns it (the first time a parry fizzles on one, the game tells you to dodge the violet); and **bronze** shots that, parried, ring out a **Resonance** that bursts the breakable shots around them, opening a hole in a wall. Earlier bosses teach two of them: Benibana's Rosary Bloom ends with a single curtain of crosses with one wide gap, and after Shirogane's Phantom Blade Rain a short fence of ghost swords falls over you with a bronze ringing blade at its heart (walk round it, or parry the blade). Against the Emperor's curtains a parry sends back at most three skulls and cuts the rest down, so parrying into a stream opens a way without filling the field with ripostes. His bullets sit on their own physics layer (`BossBullet`) so a screen full of them stays cheap.

### Bosses

Each stage ends in a two-phase boss fight with its own arena, music, title card and voiced lines. **Stance meter.** Every boss has a thin gold stance meter under its health bar. Everything you land fills it: a plain slash barely (0.5%), a parried shot a little (2%), the Spirit Shuriken more (10%), a parried boss slash a solid chunk (15%) and a parried set piece (Utsusemi's hour cleave, Kagetsune's Last Toll and Soul Coffin, Shirogane's Fang) the most (35%). Each upgrade you hold for that attack (an element blade or Sword Art for slashes, an element shuriken, an element riposte for parries) makes it fill a quarter faster. Boon effects don't fill it. When it's full the boss **staggers**: its attack is cut short, it reels for about two seconds and takes double damage, and the meter empties, flashing white. Leave it alone for three seconds and it drains. Super armour and phase breaks hold it in place, second forms resist a little, and Kagetsune resists the most. A stagger breaks a boss's stance but not an attack it has already launched: Benibana's slammed chains, Utsusemi's swinging plumb weights and its loosed Stopped Hour cleaves play out to the end.

#### Benibana, the Crimson Smoke (Stage 1)

Benibana, the court sword-dancer, is a red-haired ronin who fights with a kiseru pipe, a rosary chain and a katana. She has two 70 HP bars and blinks around the top of the screen, chaining smoke rings, a column-slamming Rosary Lash and the Crimson Iaido, a crescent that only a parry answers; parried back, it builds her stance meter. In phase 2 she fights from inside a smoke veil, wreathed in crimson flame.

### The ronin's kit

- **Katana slash.** The main attack is a katana swing that sends a crescent slash wave up the screen (the template's dart, renamed `Projectile`, carries it). The kunai is a skin for it, picked at the weapon rack: he throws a spirit-flame kunai (recoloured per element) with its own throw pose and sound, but it flies and hits exactly like the slash wave, Sword Arts and all (`Ronin.Blade`, `Ronin.Skin`). The old kunai weapon's menu and boon icons are archived unused in `Assets/Archive/Kunai/`.
- **New abilities.** The **Spirit Shuriken** is a big spinning blade that cuts through a whole column (6 s cooldown). **Parry** is a quick katana stance. A parry that deflects something is ready again at once, so well-timed parries chain; a missed parry (one that deflects nothing) has a 2 s cooldown. A parried arrow flies back as itself, wrapped in a pulsing parry aura with a trail of afterimages, and homes in on a random enemy still standing (a samurai, the scout or the boss), passing the other enemies and arrows on the way (but not your own cover, which stops it) and picking a new target if its own falls first. Both projectiles go through `Projectile`, which also handles piercing.
- **Lives.** 2 lives (the Iron Will song raises it to 4), shown as small pixel hearts in a strip along the very bottom-left, below the ronin's feet so they never hide him: a crimson heart wreathed in Oni's teal soul-fire (PixelLab), burnt out to dark slate for each one lost. The Water boon Tidal Renewal is the only way to raise the maximum (up to 5). A blocker worn down by hits has a 20% chance to drop a heart that heals one lost life; at full health the ronin can't take it, so it waits on his row until he's been hurt; at the end of a round any heart he can use flies to him and the rest fade into points. After a hit the ronin plays his hit animation, blinks and is invulnerable for 2 s. (`Ronin`, `GameUI.SetLives`)
- **Shields (courtyard blockers).** Four sets of courtyard blockers, each a bush, a stone lantern and a bush (`BlockerWall`, `Blocker`). Bushes take 5 hits and lanterns 8; each shows three damage states and then breaks into leaves or stone chips, so you can shoot gaps into a set. Samurai that march into the blockers trample them. Blockers are rebuilt every round and on every new map, and each map has its own cover, made with PixelLab in three looks per kind so no two sites match: roadside stones around clumps of bamboo on the Bamboo Road, mossy boulders around roadside Jizō statues on the Pilgrim's Pass, crimson maple shrubs around stone komainu guardians in Benibana's shrine, heaps of brass gears around clock pillars on Utsusemi's temple floor, snow-capped stones around broken torii posts on the Frozen Shrine path, frosted shrubs around snow-capped shrine lanterns in Shirogane's court, grave markers around temple bell frames on the Mountain Shrine stair, and oni-faced braziers of teal fire around broken palace pillars in the Emperor's courtyard. The original courtyard's bushes and stone lanterns (also three looks each) are the fallback for any map without its own. Each kind keeps the bush's or lantern's toughness and breaks through three damage states.

### Boons & builds

- **Elemental souls and Hades-style boons.** Fire, Frost, Storm, (once you've met Asagi, whose Water soul is the first boon reward after the meeting) Water and (once you've met Oliver, the same way) Steel souls each offer three boons for the ronin's Attack, Special, Parry and Ultimate slots, or as passives. As in Hades, a boon's rarity and its level are separate: Spirit Threads raise the rarity and Spirit Beads the level. Duos and Legendaries unlock from the boons you already hold.

- **Statuses.** Each element marks what it touches, shown as a tint. **Burning** samurai (fire blasts that don't kill them) can't hold their aim: their shots fly off at an angle, ember-red, and a veteran or Elite with more than one hit point also smoulders down a point every second and a half, but never to its last (a burn alone never kills); **Chilled** ones (Frost Blade's cold, any freeze) loose slow, frost-pale arrows; **Jolted** ones (lightning beside a strike) have their next shot fizzle in their hands; **Soaked** ones take one more from every boon effect and from your own slash; **Cracked** ones shed scrap when you cut them down. ### Meta-progression & the temple

- **Three save slots and the temple between runs, like Hades.** START opens three save slots. A new journey asks for a control scheme and walks straight into the first run; every later visit opens in the **temple**, the abandoned shrine on the hill and this game's House of Hades. Between runs Oni walks it in eight directions, and after each death he wakes in its spirit pool. Its stations are the flute altar (the Flute Songs), the memorial stones (his record, and an encyclopedia of every foe he has faced: animated figures with their story and his tally against them, unmet ones only a silhouette), the weapon rack (skins) and the gate that starts the next run.

<details><summary>The save slots and the temple in full</summary>

## Balancing

The template was easy because the player could fire 20 shots per second and the fleet never sped up. Changes:

- Player fire cooldown raised from 0.05 s to 0.35 s.
- The army speeds up as it shrinks: up to 3.5× in round 1, rising to 4.2× by round 3.
- The army grows each round (24 / 36 / 40 samurai) and starts closer to the player, archers add three-arrow fans from round 2, and Taishō add unbreakable spears in round 3. Stage 2 adds Shieldbearers and divers; Stage 3 (55 / 60 / 72 samurai) adds Yurei whose arrows ignore cover, divers from its first round and a second Taishō rank. Stage 4 (60 / 72 / 78 samurai) mixes every kind with Bellringers, divers throughout and scouts from round 2.
- Arrows come from the front line, partly aimed at the player, and get faster each round.
- **Stage 2 is the spike.** Stage 1 teaches the plain army; from Stage 2 the army starts shooting back in earnest: volleys, fast and shielded arrows, ragged formations (squads and staggered ranks), 2-hit veteran ranks and one multi-hit Elite per round, with guarded (parry-only) arrows added in Stage 3. A volley round fires less often, so the pressure climbs without the screen filling. Values per round (`GameManager.Defaults.cs`; veteran rows counted from the top, chances are fast / shielded / guarded):

  | Round | Formation | Veteran rows | Volley | Arrow chances | Elite HP |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 1-1, 1-2 | Grid | none | 1 | 0 / 0 / 0 | 4 |
  | 1-3 | Staggered | none | 1 | 0 / 0 / 0 | 4 |
  | 2-1 | 2 squads | 2 | 1 | 15 / 15 / 0% | 4 |
  | 2-2 | Staggered | 1 | 2 | 20 / 20 / 0% | 4 |
  | 2-3 | 3 squads | 2 | 2 | 20 / 25 / 0% | 4 |
  | 3-1 | Staggered | 3, 4 | 2 | 25 / 25 / 10% | 5 |
  | 3-2 | 3 squads | 2 | 2 | 25 / 30 / 12% | 5 |
  | 3-3 | Staggered | 4 | 3 | 30 / 30 / 15% | 5 |

  | 4-2 | Staggered | 3, 5 | 3 | 30 / 30 / 18% | 6 |
  | 4-3 | 3 squads | 3, 4 | 3 | 35 / 35 / 20% | 6 |

  Fast arrows fly 1.25 times the round's arrow speed, a shielded arrow takes two cuts, and the Elite's own shot comes about every 2.4 seconds at 1.1 times speed. Extra rounds repeat the last round's layout with a one-hit-tougher Elite each time. The Elite's guaranteed soul adds about twelve souls over a run, so the harder armies pay back in boons.
- The cover is rebuilt for every round, since each round is fought further along the road.
- Play-test pass: Flame Blade + Shadow Clone + Split Slash made the game trivial (fire blasts on every kill, a full-strength mirrored slash, extra side slashes, all setting off boons). Now Flame Blade only sometimes bursts and adds to a boss hit on 60% of that chance, Kindling no longer sets off Flame Blade too, Blazing Hands is 8 / 14 / 20%, Shadow Clone is an ultimate that has to be earned, and the shadow's and Split Slash's side slashes deal half to bosses and set off no boons. Storm got a little stronger (Storm Blade 45 / 60 / 80%, Thunder Shuriken 40 / 55 / 70%, Static Charge 15 / 22 / 30%, Raijin's Drum every 6th hit). Glacier Wall, which made the cover that also stops your own slashes tougher, became Glacier Shell. Utsusemi keeps his gold armour while each hour's dash takes aim, so Hour Strikes only opens up when he is down on your ground, and he recovers a little faster; Shirogane's crescents take two plain slashes to cut down.
- Benibana is meant to be the hardest part of Stage 1: dense, combo-chained patterns, an unparryable chain, and a faster second phase. Her opening comes from parrying the Iaido back at her. Shirogane is the hardest fight before the end: two 140 HP bars, lanes and fans that always leave a way through but keep moving, ice pillars to parry or dodge and glacier spires that must be dodged, a parry-only draw in each phase, and from her second phase Kuon, an 80 HP partner on the same bar that hunts on its own clock. Whichever of them falls first, the other fights on enraged. Her openings are a parried Moonsplitter, the grief stagger when Kuon falls, and the two seconds she (or enraged Kuon) stands spent after each ultimate. Kagetsune is the final exam, tuned to beat a full set of boons: two 210 HP bars, short recoveries and long combos, bullet-hell curtains that always leave a gap you can reach, timer-bomb talismans that leave you a place to stand, feints and held blows that punish dodging on rhythm, his own levies of the dead army falling in below him, and in phase 2 the other three bosses fighting beside him at 30% health. His openings are the parried Last Toll and Soul Coffin, a summoned soul cut down (its soul hammers his stance), and the moment he kneels after the Night of a Thousand Bells; an opening doubles damage, but not on top of a stagger's own double.
- The game is still beatable thanks to the lives, invulnerability after a hit, and the courtyard blockers.
- A fresh start is deliberately a little weaker than the tuning above (2 lives, a slightly slower walk and slash), as in Hades, where the first deaths buy Thick Skin and Death Defiance. The Flute Songs win it back and go a little past it: up to 4 lives, a revive, quicker feet, slash and tools, a wider parry and more control over the rewards. The last ranks wait on Warlord's Seals and Kite Silk ([ECONOMY.md](Docs/ECONOMY.md)).
- Because the base game is hard, souls give the player a way to build up power over a run. The paths, scouts and the end of each stage all give boons, and the Spirit Shuriken and Parry give tools for clearing columns and dealing with arrows. The rounds per stage, when the boss path opens, how many paths open, and the extra rounds' speed-up are tunable on `GameManager`.

All of these values can be tuned in the Inspector on `GameManager` (the `stages` array, with each stage's `rounds`), `SamuraiArmy`, `Ronin` and `BlockerWall`. Benibana's timings are in `Benibana.cs`, Utsusemi's in `Utsusemi.cs`, Shirogane's in `Shirogane.cs`, Kagetsune's in `Kagetsune.cs`.

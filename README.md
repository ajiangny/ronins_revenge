# Ronin's Revenge — Assignment 1

A Space Invaders remake re-themed as a lone chibi ronin holding a moonlit temple courtyard against marching samurai armies.

Built on the class template ([Oren-Steinberg/Space-Invaders-3D](https://github.com/Oren-Steinberg/Space-Invaders-3D)), Unity 6000.6, URP.
Open `Assets/Scenes/SampleScene.unity` and press Play.

**Controls:** two schemes, picked on two cards (KEYBOARD / KEYBOARD + MOUSE, each listing its keys as drawn caps, with mouse buttons and the spacebar as icons) when a new journey starts, changeable under SETTINGS or on the CONTROLS page (PLAY WITH), and switched automatically mid-fight when you use the other one's buttons (a mouse click, or a Keyboard-only attack key); every key prompt (Tab tips, Poguru's lessons, the Controls and Tips pages) shows the scheme in use, and a lesson's key caps redraw if you switch mid-lesson.
- **Keyboard:** one hand moves, the other attacks, in either of two layouts that work together. Arcade: the arrows move, Space slashes (hold), C parries, X throws the Spirit Shuriken, Z unleashes the ultimate. Or A/D move with J slash, K parry, L shuriken, I ultimate.
- **Keyboard + Mouse:** A/D move, hold Left Click to slash, Right Click to parry, Space for the Spirit Shuriken, E (or Middle Click) for the ultimate.

In the temple WASD or the arrows walk anywhere and E uses a station. Esc or P to pause (in a browser's fullscreen, where Esc may only leave fullscreen, P pauses and leaving fullscreen mid-fight pauses too). Menus: Up/Down (or W/S) to select, Left/Right to adjust a slider, Enter or Space to confirm, Esc to go back; the mouse and a gamepad work too. On the boon screen: Up/Down (or W/S, or 1/2/3) to select, Enter or click to choose, R to redraw and B to banish once the Flute Songs for them are learned, and X twice (or a click on the hint) to reject every offer and take nothing. Between rounds: walk under a path (a tooltip under it says what it gives) and press E or Enter to choose it. Enter or Space skips a boss's intro. For testing (Editor and development builds, and release builds while the GameManager's "Dev Tools In Release" box is ticked, as it is for friends' play-test builds), DEV TOOLS on the title and pause menus (or F3) starts any stage or boss fight directly and toggles god mode (also F4); F1 clears the current army, F2 jumps to the last boss, F5 drops a soul (Fire, Frost and Storm in turn), F6 drops a technique scroll, F9 charges the ultimate, and F8 prints the balancing run log (also written to `runlog.txt` in the game's data folder).

The template's Space Invaders classes, prefabs and layers are renamed to the theme: `Alien` → `Samurai`, `AlienFleet` → `SamuraiArmy`, `AlienBullet` → `Arrow`, `AlienCounter` → `SamuraiCounter`, `Spaceship` → `Ronin`, `Dart` → `Projectile`, `ShieldWall`/`ShieldBlock` → `BlockerWall`/`Blocker`. The section on template code issues below uses the template's original names.

## Required features

| Requirement | Where |
|---|---|
| Remaining aliens speed up as others are destroyed | `SamuraiArmy.CurrentSpeed()` scales linearly from `baseSpeed` to `baseSpeed × maxSpeedMultiplier` as the army shrinks. |
| Detect when the game can no longer be won | In the original, an army that lands ends the game. Here the army stops descending once its front rank reaches the cover's row (`SamuraiArmy.holdDepth`) and walks that row, smashing the cover, while its front rank lunges at the ronin, so the run goes on as a last stand. `ArmyReachedRonin` (an army at the ronin's head, `Ronin.TopY`, which the hold line keeps it from reaching) still ends the game as a safeguard. |
| Ship can't move offscreen | `Ronin.FixedUpdate()` clamps the ronin to the camera's visible width, using his sprite's measured bounds (the pivot isn't centred on the art). |
| Balance (harder but beatable) | See [Balancing](#balancing). |

## Code issues found in the template and fixed

1. **Dynamic Rigidbodies were moved by writing `transform.position` every physics step.** This fights the physics engine and teleports colliders. Aliens and the ship are now kinematic and move with `Rigidbody.MovePosition` (interpolated). Darts and alien bullets are launched once with `linearVelocity`.
2. **Projectiles destroyed whatever they hit (`Destroy(coll.gameObject)`).** An alien bullet would delete the ship, a shield, or anything else outright, which made lives and shields impossible to add. Now each object handles its own collision: `Alien` dies, `Spaceship` loses a life, `ShieldBlock` takes damage, and the projectile only destroys itself. `Dart.TryConsume()` keeps one dart from killing two aliens in the same step.
3. **The UI was polled and rebuilt every frame.** `AlienFleet.Update` copied the count into `AlienCounter`, and `AlienCounter.Update` rebuilt the string every frame, allocating garbage. The count is now pushed only when it changes (`AlienCounter.SetCount`, using TMP's non-allocating `SetText`).
4. **Every alien rolled a random number to fire on every `FixedUpdate`.** That's N random checks per step, back-row aliens shooting through their own fleet, and a `static` cooldown that every alien shared. The fleet now fires one shot on a timer from the bottom alien of a column, sometimes aimed at the player's column.
5. **The fleet's direction change relied on `Invoke("startSidewaysMotion", …)`.** It used a string method name (not refactor-safe, not camelCase), took two passes over the alien list, and started the bounds at `0` instead of ±infinity. It's replaced with a fixed drop distance tracked in `dropRemaining` and a single combined move-and-measure loop. Hard-coded `±16` bounds are replaced with bounds taken from the camera.
6. **Leftover clutter and broken components.** The template had redundant `fleet.GetComponent<AlienFleet>()` calls on a field already typed `AlienFleet`, empty `Start`/`Update` methods (Unity still calls them), and commented-out dead code. Tuning values were public statics, so they didn't show in the Inspector; they're now `[SerializeField]`. The Spaceship also had **two** Rigidbodies (one in the prefab, one added in the scene) and a `MeshCollider` with no mesh. The `List<GameObject>` is now a typed `List<Alien>`.

## Additional features (four or more required)

- **Story.** The ronin is **Oni**, once Emperor Kagetsune's guard. Kagetsune betrayed him on the Night of a Thousand Bells, sealing his court, his army and Oni's village into an armor that makes him deathless. Every enemy is a soul from that armor sent out in a borrowed body, which is why they come back each night and remember. Oni's soul tore free, but it can't rest while the armor holds the people he led there, so every death pulls him back to the abandoned temple on the hill where he used to play her flute, and he wakes in its spirit pool. Each boss is a victim too: Benibana, the court sword-dancer whose smoke he used and whose people he sealed; Utsusemi, the monk who sheltered Oni, wound into clockwork; and Shirogane, Oni's sworn partner, who saved his soul at the cost of her own. Their intro, phase and defeat lines tell it. The story, a file for each character and a Hades-style reactive dialogue design are in [Story/](Story/README.md).

- **Reactive dialogue, the Hades way.** The game remembers every run (a save in `PlayerPrefs`: deaths and wins against each boss, what killed you last time, which lines you've heard) and picks what each character says from conversations written for situations. The first time you meet a boss you hear its full intro; die to Benibana and next time she opens with "Back so soon?"; after three deaths to her she counts them; beat her twice and she tells you which bell is hers, which Shirogane later answers. Story lines play once and in order, reactions rest a few runs before they repeat, and every boss keeps short repeatable lines drawn so nothing repeats until all have played. The boss who kills you has the last word, and as he climbs out of the temple's pool Oni says a few words after a death, or the soul he leaned on most that run does. Poguru then roasts whatever killed you, down to what struck the last blow: an arrow ("The thing you cut in half for FUN?"), a Taisho's spear, a ghost arrow through cover, a diving soldier, the army marching all the way down, or each boss, with escalating lines for dying the same way again; now and then Oni snaps back or just grunts. At his nest Poguru does an impression of Oni's flat "...No." when Oni turns him down. The bosses react to which souls you carry (Kagetsune sneers at the village drum), and the souls meet Poguru and talk over each other. F7 (dev tools) prints what each speaker could say and why the rest can't play. Lines not yet recorded show as subtitles. (`Dialogue.cs`, `SaveData.cs`, research in [Story/DIALOGUE_RESEARCH.md](Story/DIALOGUE_RESEARCH.md))
- **Talk scenes, like Hades.** Conversations play as scenes over the frozen field: the speaker's painting stands large at the left, Oni's at the right, and the line types into a dark plate under the speaker's name and title on a brush-ink tab; whoever isn't talking drops back into shadow, and Space, Enter or a click moves on. Every boss fight opens with one after the boss's title card and ends with one as the boss falls, and the first time you meet a boss, break its phase or beat it, Oni answers. A phase break gets a scene only when there's something new to say; otherwise its card speaks the line. The first time each soul (Akagane of Fire, Mizore of Frost, Hibiki of Storm) offers you its boons in a run, it talks to Oni before the cards appear: a chain of first meetings told one per run, reactions to how your last run ended and what you hold, and short greetings. As in Hades, each soul announces itself with its own sting: Akagane a war-drum boom and roaring flame, Mizore ice chimes over a singing bowl, Hibiki a taiko drumroll into thunder. A Legendary on offer gets its own lines, and a Duo plays both souls bickering; they mark the boss just felled or the one ahead, and sulk when another soul's element leads, with the rival answering back. Every line is voiced. The three souls were painted in the bosses' portrait style with GPT image generation. During play, lines show as plain subtitles with no portrait. (`DialogueScene`, `Dialogue.cs`)

- **Spirit Notes and the Flute Songs (meta-progression).** Like Hades' Mirror of Night or Vampire Survivors' PowerUps, every kill earns Spirit Notes that are kept when you die (1 a samurai, 5 a scout, 25 to 100 a boss): each freed soul leaves behind a note of the songs Shirogane taught Oni. In the temple they're counted in the bottom-right corner with their icon, where any later resources stack up too, as in Hades' House (`ResourceTray`). They're spent at the flute altar in the temple: small stat songs (a wider parry window, faster feet, swing and shuriken, longer invulnerability, a fourth life, and Second Dawn, which raises Oni once a run) and songs that give control over a run's rewards rather than power: Third Path (three paths after each round), Fate's Thread (up to three boon redraws a run, R), Banishing Bell (B takes a boon out of the run), Pond's Blessing (a second Spirit Bead after the first boss) and Ancestral Lore (each stage's first soul is Rare or better). A fresh save plays exactly like the tuned game, so the first run is beatable; the songs only add on top, about twice the survivability and no flat damage, the same size Hades and Vampire Survivors land on. Prices climb Vampire Survivors-style, with a refund and a switch to turn maxed songs off. (`Upgrades.cs`, design and research in [ECONOMY.md](Docs/ECONOMY.md))
- **The Oath Stone (Heat), after the first dawn.** In the temple's lower-left corner stands a stone bound in chains; once Kagetsune has fallen the chains break (they lie snapped at its foot from then on, and the vows carved into it glow ember-red) and, like Hades' Pact of Punishment, it takes oaths that make every run harder: faster armies, more and faster arrows, tougher warlords, the boss path opening later, no Spirit Beads down the paths, one life fewer. Each rank sworn is a point of Heat, which raises the Spirit Notes earned by 10% a point, and the first dawn at a new highest Heat pays a bounty (150 notes per point). The Memorial stones show the highest Heat cleared. (`Oaths.cs`)

- **Three save slots and the temple between runs, like Hades.** START opens CHOOSE A JOURNEY: three slots, each showing its runs, notes, deaths and dawns, and ERASE A JOURNEY (with a confirm). An empty slot is a new game: it first asks HOW WILL YOU FIGHT? on two cards, keyboard only or keyboard and mouse, so the tutorial shows the right keys; then the ronin puts his flute away, stands and walks into the first run, as Hades' first run starts straight in Tartarus. A journey already begun opens in the **temple**, the abandoned shrine on the hill, the House of Hades of this game. It's a PixelLab map with its gate at the top and a glowing spirit pool at the bottom, its stations built into the courtyard: the flute altar on a stone dais between two lit toro lanterns, the memorial stones in a kerbed grave nook behind their offerings, the weapon rack on a plank platform under a broken eave, and the oath stone on the flagstones below the dais, a carved standing stone wrapped in rusted, padlocked chains. Incense rises off the altar, light glints on the blades, lantern light flickers on the stones and ghost-fires flicker at the courtyard's edges. Oni walks behind and around the props and the stone lanterns, and takes the steps up to the altar's dais and the side step onto the rack's platform. The pool is alive: light plays on the water, rings spread on it and little beady-eyed souls drift up out of it and off the flagstones all around; when Oni steps in he wades, the water hiding his feet, rings following his steps, splashing in and out (`SoulPool`). It is also the only place Oni can walk in every direction (WASD or the arrows, in eight directions: his PixelLab run sideways and an eight-frame walk up, down and on each diagonal; when he stops he stays facing the way he was going, breathing on an idle drawn for each of the eight directions). There is no game-over screen: when Oni dies, the boss that killed him has its last word, the dark comes down and he wakes in the spirit pool like Zagreus rising from the Styx, climbs out as the soul-fire flares, sees what the run earned, and sometimes says something. Walk up to a station and press E: the **flute altar** (the Flute Songs, Hades' Mirror of Night), the **memorial stones** (runs, dawns and deaths, and each warlord he has met by how often they've killed each other; unmet ones stay worn smooth), the **weapon rack** (Hades' Infernal Arms: the katana, or the kunai as a skin for it, kept in the save) and the **gate**, which sets out on the next run. The gate works: its two doors swing open with a creak as Oni walks up to it and shut behind him when he walks away, and setting out he walks through the doorway into the dark road beyond and under the gate's beam, which hides him as he goes. Victory and the pause menu's RETURN TO TEMPLE bring him back the same way. Safe areas are data (a map, a walkable floor, a wake spot and a list of stations), so more can be added later. (`SafeArea`, `SaveData.cs`, `GameManager.EnterSafeArea`)
- **Poguru, the Lost Dragon, and a first-run tutorial.** A chubby baby dragon of teal soul-fire with a scrap of red festival cape lives in Oni's left spirit wisp (pixel frames from PixelLab, a painted portrait, his own ElevenLabs voice). On a new journey he pops out of the wisp before the first army arrives and asks "Do you remember how to fight?": TUTORIAL or SKIP. The tutorial is Stage I, Round 1 itself. First five dead soldiers find Oni: an archer, whose arrow freezes mid-air until the player slashes it (the cut breaks it), then must be cut down; two chargers in a file, frozen until one shuriken goes through both; and a spearman whose cursed spear (Kagetsune's blacksmiths' work: it can't be cut or broken) freezes until it's parried. At each freeze Poguru points at that ability's HUD icon with its keys beside it as key caps (mouse buttons drawn as a mouse), and only that move lets the game go on; Poguru's yell breaks off the moment the move is made. The spearman and a cowardly fifth soldier flee for the army, which marches in ("Uh oh. He brought a LOT of backup."). Poguru headbutts away every arrow at Oni for the whole round, so no life is lost, getting redder and crosser by the bonk ("My HEAD is not a SHIELD!") but never giving up. Partway through the round he pours some of his soul-fire into the ultimate's medallion ("Here, have some of my soul-fire!"), freezes the field, points at it with its key (Z, or E with the mouse) ("Let your SHADOW out!"), and the Shadow Clone fights the rest of the round at Oni's side ("Two Onis! It copies everything you do. Just weaker. Like me!"). If the army reaches the cover his rage bursts out as a ring of soul-fire that wipes it out, and he yells at Oni for napping instead of celebrating. Nothing drops during the tutorial. Then a homing spear he can't stop goes straight through him and takes a life (even in god mode), so he explains the hearts in the corner and drops a heart on Oni's row to win it back; then he reacts to the round's soul, explains the boon cards over the first boon screen (picking is locked while he talks) and the paths, and goes back in the wisp as round 2 begins. A death mid-tutorial offers it again next run. Esc pauses it anywhere, even mid-lesson or mid-conversation (its lines and voices hold), with RESUME, SKIP TUTORIAL, CONTROLS, SETTINGS, QUIT TO TITLE and QUIT GAME (not in the web build); SKIP TUTORIAL fades to black and restarts the run as a regular Round 1. Outside the tutorial he pops out for short reactive barks (a round starting, the first boon, a boss falling, waking in the temple), and in the temple he sits on his nest, a cracked offering bowl right of the pool, with TALK (a Hades-style conversation once a visit), TIPS and a REPLAY TUTORIAL switch. Soldiers now very rarely drop a heart while Oni is hurt, as Poguru says they do.
- **Menus and start/end screens.** A title screen laid out like a Final Fantasy title: the Ronin's Revenge logo centred high on a paper-white page, a short left-aligned menu column under it (Start / Controls / Settings / Quit; Start opens the save slots) with the selection marked by a red diamond and red ink, and the ronin sitting on his row in the bottom-left corner playing his flute, little pixel music notes in his spirit teal drifting up from it (his idle stands in until the flute frames are drawn). Starting a new journey fades the logo and menu away; the ronin puts his flute away and gets to his feet (a PixelLab stand-up clip), then walks out to the middle of his row while the paper page fades into the Bamboo Road under him, and the first stage card paints on. Any key skips it. Pages opened from the title (the save slots, the control choice, Controls, Settings, Dev Tools) stay on its white page in ink, the ronin still playing his flute in the corner; opened from the pause menu, they sit over the dimmed fight. Controls shows each action with its keys drawn as key caps plus the rules in three lines; Settings has Music and Sound volume sliders that apply instantly and are saved with `PlayerPrefs`. Esc pauses mid-fight (Resume / Return to Temple / Restart / Controls / Settings / Quit to Title). Before each round a stage card paints on across the middle of the field in the boss cards' style: a stroke of dry-brush ink, a small "Stage I · Round 1" overline, the stage's name in large Roman capitals, a brush rule with a diamond in the round's colour, and a line of text, each rising in turn, while the army marches in above and below it. The Victory screen shows score, high score and how far you got, calls out a new high score, and offers Return to Temple / Title; a death goes straight back to the temple. The selected menu row is marked by a red diamond, a slight scale-up and, over the dimmed fight, a stroke of red ink that paints on behind it, with sounds on move and confirm. High score is saved with `PlayerPrefs`. (`GameManager`, `MenuUI`, `GameUI`)
- **Readable UI.** Following the Xbox Accessibility Guidelines and Game Accessibility Guidelines: no text below 26px at 1080p, every piece of text outlined and drop-shadowed so it reads over the busy playfield, the decorative brush font kept to titles with a clean font for everything else, and no italics. The HUD keeps a minimal arcade layout (score in a single row top left; the stage number top right with the round on a smaller line beneath it, right-aligned; a small dim label beside each number; lives bottom left) on a soft shade, leaving the top centre free for a compact boss bar that sits above the bosses rather than over them. Boss lines show on a dark plate with the speaker's name. (`GameUI.Style`, `TextStyle`)
- **Additional levels: stages of rounds.** The game is built from stages (`StageConfig`), each a run of rounds (`LevelConfig`) followed by a boss, configured in `GameManager.stages`. Stage 1, **The Bamboo Road**, has three configured rounds and then Benibana. Each round brings a bigger army (8×3, then 9×4, then 10×4) that starts lower, marches faster, and shoots more often with more aimed, faster arrows. Each new army marches down into the field from above the screen to the drum while the stage card shows, and it can't be hit until the round starts. The blockers are rebuilt only at the start of a stage, so the damage they take carries through all its rounds. Beating a stage earns a boon and a Spirit Bead, and the HUD shows the stage and round.
- **Paths between rounds, like Hades' chamber doors.** Each round is fought for a reward, named on its banner and given when the army falls: a stage's first round always ends in a soul. When the army falls, any drops still out fly to the ronin first: hearts heal him up to full, and any he can't use fade away into points (250 × the stage), so none linger into the next round. Then the round's reward gathers out of the air in the middle of the field, hangs there a moment, and is pulled into him before its screen opens. Then two paths open where the army stood, each showing what the next round will give (a Fire, Frost or Storm soul, a Spirit Bead, a Lost Melody of Spirit Notes, a healing spring while hurt, or a technique scroll). The ronin walks his row; a path lights only while he stands under it (between them a small "Walk under a path." prompt rides over his head, and nothing can be chosen), and a small tooltip hangs under its name saying what it gives (a Fire Soul: "Receive a boon from a lost soul."). Choosing one sends its reward floating away up the field, to come back as that round's reward, and Oni sets off after it into the next round. From round 3 the warlord's path opens beside them, but you can keep going: a stage runs up to 5 rounds, the extra ones repeating its last army faster, with more arrows, a commander rank and, in round 5, one more rank. Going deeper builds a stronger ronin at the cost of more risk before the boss. Each path shows its reward as animated pixel art (PixelLab): the souls as their paintings on the boon screen show them, each flame wisp over its bell (Akagane's bronze bell, Mizore's silver one, Hibiki's red cord of tin bells), a glowing prayer bead, a pair of teal spirit notes, a bubbling pink spring, a tied steel-blue scroll, and for the warlord's path something of theirs: Benibana's kiseru with her rosary wound round it, smoke curling up; Utsusemi's great hat with its slit-eye glowing beneath; the head of Kuon, Shirogane's spirit wolf; and Kagetsune's horned helm hung with bells. Taking a bead or a scroll opens its own screen, painted like the soul screens: Oni holds out a painted prayer bead or technique scroll the way he holds a soul, and on every boon screen a slow vortex of the element's light turns behind what he holds. (`RewardPath.cs`, `RewardSpirit.cs`, `GameManager.ChoosePath`, `SwirlUI`)
- **Samurai kinds: an army of the dead.** The army is the dead of the Night of a Thousand Bells, sent back out each night in the bodies they died in: rotted armour and bone, a faint teal ember of soul in each eye, crumbling to bones and ash when cut down ([Story/Army.md](Story/Army.md)). Rows take their kind from `LevelConfig.rowKinds` (`SamuraiKind`). **Ashigaru**, skeletal foot soldiers in rotted straw hats, loose single arrows. **Archers**, corpse bushi in cracked lamellar, loose a fan of three arrows and are worth 30 points. **Taishō**, revenant generals in horned kabuto with cracked menpō over bare skulls, are the top tier: they take two hits, are worth 60 points, and every 3 to 6 seconds one of them hurls a **heavy spear** over its own ranks. The spear can't be destroyed: slash waves and arrows break on it and it cuts through cover, so the only answers are to dodge it or parry it. A parried spear flies back up as a riposte that runs through three samurai. Archers first appear in the top row in round 2; in round 3 the Taishō hold the top row with archers below them. (`Samurai`, `SamuraiArmy`, `Arrow.MakeUnbreakable`)
- **Kite scouts (the mystery ship).** In round 3 a kite-borne scout, a shrouded corpse lashed to a torn paper kite, drifts across the top of the screen every 15 to 25 seconds. One hit brings it down for 100, 150 or 300 bonus points (multiplied by the stage number) and a guaranteed soul. (`Scout`)
- **Stage 2: The Pilgrim's Pass.** A blue-clan army. Round 1 adds a front rank of **Shieldbearers** (hulking dead men behind temple doors nailed with iron and ofuda; two hits, never fire) that the archers shoot over. Round 2 adds **divers**: every few seconds a samurai from behind the front rank flashes, cries out and swoops down at the ronin, smashing through cover, before gliding back into formation. Round 3 mixes everything with scouts. (`SamuraiKind.Shieldbearer`, `LevelConfig.diveInterval`, `SamuraiArmy`)
- **Stage 3: The Frozen Shrine.** A pale, frostbitten army on a snowbound shrine path, and the hardest rounds of the run: 11×5, 12×5 and then 12×6 samurai starting lower and marching faster, with divers from round 1, scouts from round 2, and two Taishō ranks in round 3. The new kind is the **Yurei**: onryō in white burial kimono with long black hair over their faces and no feet, the souls whose bodies are gone, drawn see-through over a spirit glow that breathes in and out. A yurei looses a parted pair of **spirit arrows that drift straight through cover**, so the blockers only shelter you from the living, and it's worth 35 points. (`SamuraiKind.Yurei`, `Samurai.Fire`, `GameManager.DefaultStages`; the stages are copied into the scene by `Ninja/Apply Stages`)
- **Stage 4: The Mountain Shrine.** The stair up to the hall where the Night of a Thousand Bells was held, under a starless sky, and the last and hardest rounds: 12×5, 12×6 and then 13×6 samurai in crimson and bronze, every kind at once (Taishō, archers, yurei, shieldbearers) with divers throughout and scouts from round 2. The new kind is the **Bellringer**, a dead shrine watchman with his jaw bound in cloth and a bronze hand bell: it never shoots and takes two hits, but every few seconds one of them glows and **tolls, and the whole army fires twice as fast** for a moment. Cut one down before its bell rings and the toll never comes; every bell you silence chimes softly and delays the next toll. It's worth 50 points. (`SamuraiKind.Bellringer`, `SamuraiArmy.UpdateBells`, `Samurai.WarnToll`/`Toll`)
- **Boss: Benibana, the Crimson Smoke.** After Stage 1's last round, the music cuts out, smoke rolls across the top of the screen, a blood moon turns the courtyard red, and Benibana descends. She's a red-haired ronin in a torn black kimono who fights with a kiseru pipe, a rosary chain and a katana. Her entrance is a cinematic in the style of a Final Fantasy XIV boss card: on a dark stage lit crimson, her full-body portrait fades up and drifts closer, then cross-fades to her close-up while a lower-third title rises in (her epithet, a thin rule drawing out from the centre, and her name, in Roman capitals), and a talk scene follows where she speaks her voiced line. She has two 70 HP bars, shown on a boss bar; the second fills in after her phase break. She blinks around the top of the screen and chains moves into combos with short recoveries:
  - **Kiseru Smoke Rings:** volleys of slow rings that swell as they fall, with each volley filling the gaps left by the last.
  - **Rosary Lash:** a wide red band marks a column and narrows to a line over about a second, then her chain slams down it, smashing blockers. It hangs there as a wall that can't be parried, and then its crosses break off and fall.
  - **Crimson Iaido:** a red line flashes, then she draws and a crescent slash sweeps down at the ronin, followed by a second draw aimed where he dodged. The crescent can only be parried: slashes, the shuriken and cover don't stop it. **Parry it on time and it flies back at her**, shattering any Iaido crescent in its path. It deals no damage itself, but it fills 15% of her stance meter, building toward a broken stance that leaves her open to double damage.
  - **Phase 2 (first bar emptied):** in a short cutscene, invincible while her HP bar refills, she staggers, draws on her pipe as the smoke rushes back into her and a crimson glow builds, the music falls away for a breath, and then her blade comes out in a crossing crimson cut that tears her kimono and blows the smoke off her: she stands revealed in her new form (sprite and all), and a second title card plays, **The Crimson Unsealed**: her new full-body painting (hair loose, tattoos burning, katana drawn) cross-fades to a close-up while she speaks a furious line. She exhales a smoke veil over the top of the screen and fights from inside it, with only her glowing eyes showing. Fire boons burn holes in the veil. She's wreathed in crimson flames and fights faster, with three-move combos, extra chains, an X-shaped Iaido, crosses hidden in her smoke rings, and the **Rosary Bloom**: three rotating waves of crosses across the whole field.
  - Boon effects work on her too: fire bursts, frost freezes her (and a slash on her ice shatters it for extra damage), and lightning arcs into her. When she falls she kneels and fades, the blood moon lifts, and her soul becomes the stage's boon. - **New abilities.** The **Spirit Shuriken** is a big spinning blade that cuts through a whole column (6 s cooldown). **Parry** is a quick katana stance. A parry that deflects something is ready again at once, so well-timed parries chain; a missed parry (one that deflects nothing) has a 2 s cooldown. A parried arrow flies back as itself, wrapped in a pulsing parry aura with a trail of afterimages, and homes in on a random enemy still standing (a samurai, the scout or the boss), passing the other enemies and arrows on the way (but not your own cover, which stops it) and picking a new target if its own falls first. Both projectiles go through `Projectile`, which also handles piercing.
- **Bullet-hell rules, taught boss by boss.** You only move left and right, so every falling enemy shot (arrows included) marks the spot where it will land on your row, a glow in its own colour that brightens as it nears (`ArrowShadow`). Patterns are built so that wherever they cross your row they leave a gap nearly twice your width, and the next gap is never further than you can comfortably run before it lands (`BulletHell`). Three kinds of shot recur: ordinary shots you can parry; **crimson** shots no parry turns (the first time a parry fizzles on one, the game tells you to dodge the crimson); and **bronze** shots that, parried, ring out a **Resonance** that bursts the breakable shots around them, opening a hole in a wall. Earlier bosses teach two of them: Benibana's Rosary Bloom ends with a single curtain of crosses with one wide gap, and after Shirogane's Phantom Blade Rain a short fence of ghost swords falls over you with a bronze ringing blade at its heart (walk round it, or parry the blade). Against the Emperor's curtains a parry sends back at most three skulls and cuts the rest down, so parrying into a stream opens a way without filling the field with ripostes. His bullets sit on their own physics layer (`BossBullet`) so a screen full of them stays cheap.

The kunai is a skin for it, picked at the weapon rack: he throws a spirit-flame kunai (recoloured per element) with its own throw pose and sound, but it flies and hits exactly like the slash wave, Sword Arts and all (`Ronin.Blade`, `Ronin.Skin`). The old kunai weapon's menu and boon icons are archived unused in `Assets/Archive/Kunai/`.
- **New background.** Stage 1, the Bamboo Road, is a strict top-down dirt road with cart ruts and fallen bamboo leaves, with the bamboo grove kept to thin strips at the very edges so nothing tall breaks the top-down view of the marching army (`Backdrop`). The original moonlit grass courtyard is kept for a later stage. It's top-down to match the 3/4-view character sprites, so the armies stand on the ground instead of floating in front of a sky.
- **Atmosphere.** Every map has its own mood laid over it, built in code (`Atmosphere`): on the Bamboo Road big dappled cloud shadows slide down the road and darken everything they pass over, soldiers, the ronin and cover included, moonlight falls from the moon's corner and the far side sinks into shadow, and fireflies drift over the field; the Pilgrim's Pass has quicker clouds and low mist rolling over the stones; Benibana's shrine glows red under her blood moon with embers in the air; Utsusemi's temple has still air, dust hanging in a cold shaft of moonlight and a creeping floor mist; the Frozen Shrine path has heavy slow snow clouds, mist in the hollows and snowflakes in the air, and Shirogane's court is dead still, spirit light pooling on the frost under a thick cyan mist and drifting spirit motes. Hit sparks, slashes and boss effects stay bright over the shadows, and the mist and moonlight lie under the characters, so the fight stays readable. The Mountain Shrine and the Emperor's courtyard have no moonlight at all: heavy dark at the edges, crimson smoke low on the ground and teal souls drifting in the air, until the dawn warms it all at the end.
- **Maps and travel between them.** Each boss has its own arena and each stage has its own ground, all generated with PixelLab in the courtyard's style: Benibana waits in a shrine court strewn with crimson maple leaves, Stage 2 is fought on the weathered stone and gravel of the Pilgrim's Pass, Utsusemi waits on a steel-blue temple floor inlaid with brass clockwork, Stage 3 climbs a snowbound shrine path between stone lanterns and snow-laden pines, and Shirogane waits in a frozen shrine court glazed with ice, a ritual ring of frost-rimmed tiles glowing cyan at its middle, under a spirit-blue moon that turns the snow to drifting light. Before a boss, and before the next stage, the ronin walks off the top of the screen (on a back-facing walk cycle, also made in PixelLab, recoloured for each element), the screen fades to black, and the new map fades in with fresh cover as he walks up into place from below. (`GameManager.TravelTo`, `Ronin.WalkTo`, `Backdrop.Show`, `StageConfig.roundsMap`/`bossMap`, wired by `Ninja/Apply Maps`) Stage 4 climbs a dark stone shrine stair between black pines, stone lanterns and broken torii, and Kagetsune waits in a black coronation courtyard around a great bronze imperial crest, fallen bells and teal braziers under a starless sky.

## Balancing

The template was easy because the player could fire 20 shots per second and the fleet never sped up. Changes:

- Player fire cooldown raised from 0.05 s to 0.35 s.
- The army speeds up as it shrinks: up to 3.5× in round 1, rising to 4.2× by round 3.
- The army grows each round (24 / 36 / 40 samurai) and starts closer to the player, archers add three-arrow fans from round 2, and Taishō add unbreakable spears in round 3. Stage 2 adds Shieldbearers and divers; Stage 3 (55 / 60 / 72 samurai) adds Yurei whose arrows ignore cover, divers from its first round and a second Taishō rank. Stage 4 (60 / 72 / 78 samurai) mixes every kind with Bellringers, divers throughout and scouts from round 2.
- Arrows come from the front line, partly aimed at the player, and get faster each round.
- The cover is rebuilt for every round, since each round is fought further along the road.
- Play-test pass: Flame Blade + Shadow Clone + Split Slash made the game trivial (fire blasts on every kill, a full-strength mirrored slash, extra side slashes, all setting off boons). Now Flame Blade only sometimes bursts and adds to a boss hit on 60% of that chance, Kindling no longer sets off Flame Blade too, Blazing Hands is 8 / 14 / 20%, Shadow Clone is an ultimate that has to be earned, and the shadow's and Split Slash's side slashes deal half to bosses and set off no boons. Storm got a little stronger (Storm Blade 45 / 60 / 80%, Thunder Shuriken 40 / 55 / 70%, Static Charge 15 / 22 / 30%, Raijin's Drum every 6th hit). Glacier Wall, which made the cover that also stops your own slashes tougher, became Glacier Shell. Utsusemi keeps his gold armour while each hour's dash takes aim, so Hour Strikes only opens up when he is down on your ground, and he recovers a little faster; Shirogane's crescents take two plain slashes to cut down.
- Benibana is meant to be the hardest part of Stage 1: dense, combo-chained patterns, an unparryable chain, and a faster second phase. Her opening comes from parrying the Iaido back at her. Shirogane is the hardest fight before the end: two 140 HP bars, lanes and fans that always leave a way through but keep moving, ice pillars to parry or dodge and glacier spires that must be dodged, a parry-only draw in each phase, and from her second phase Kuon, an 80 HP partner on the same bar that hunts on its own clock. Whichever of them falls first, the other fights on enraged. Her openings are a parried Moonsplitter, the grief stagger when Kuon falls, and the two seconds she (or enraged Kuon) stands spent after each ultimate. Kagetsune is the final exam, tuned to beat a full set of boons: two 210 HP bars, short recoveries and long combos, bullet-hell curtains that always leave a gap you can reach, a seal that takes away your parry, feints and held blows that punish dodging on rhythm, his own levies of the dead army falling in below him, and in phase 2 the other three bosses fighting beside him at 30% health. His openings are the parried Last Toll and Soul Coffin, a summoned soul cut down (its soul hammers his stance), and the moment he kneels after the Night of a Thousand Bells; an opening doubles damage, but not on top of a stagger's own double.
- The game is still beatable thanks to 3 lives, invulnerability after a hit, and the courtyard blockers.
- The Flute Songs never weaken a fresh start: all of the above is the tuning a first run gets. Bought songs add at most a life, a revive, about 10-15% quicker tools and more control over the rewards, so later runs get easier without the first one being made harder ([ECONOMY.md](Docs/ECONOMY.md)).
- Because the base game is hard, souls give the player a way to build up power over a run. The paths, scouts and the end of each stage all give boons, and the Spirit Shuriken and Parry give tools for clearing columns and dealing with arrows. The rounds per stage, when the boss path opens, how many paths open, and the extra rounds' speed-up are tunable on `GameManager`.

All of these values can be tuned in the Inspector on `GameManager` (the `stages` array, with each stage's `rounds`), `SamuraiArmy`, `Ronin` and `BlockerWall`. Benibana's timings are in `Benibana.cs`, Utsusemi's in `Utsusemi.cs`, Shirogane's in `Shirogane.cs`, Kagetsune's in `Kagetsune.cs`.

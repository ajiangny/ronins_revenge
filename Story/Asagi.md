| | |
|---|---|

*Asagi* is a koi variety: blue net-patterned scales on a white body, with orange on the cheeks. The word also means
pale blue. Her koi is an Asagi. She is named after herself.

## Look

**The painting.** Her talk-scene portrait (`Assets/Art/Portraits/Asagi_Closeup.png`, with `Asagi_Full.png`) is
painted from this brief in the boss portraits' style (GPT image gen), from the user's references: a figure in a
suit with a glass bowl helmet, an Asagi koi, a cobalt-rimmed ruffled glass vase, and an elderly physician in a long
coat holding a rack of vials.

- **The head is a glass bowl.** Round, clear, full of water, with a ruffled cobalt lip like a vase. Her koi swims
  inside and looks out at you, and she is plainly a soul, not a pet: half-translucent and lit from within, blue net
  scales glowing faintly on a pale body you can almost see through, a white face, orange cheek patches like embers,
  fins and tail trailing off into wisps of soft sea-blue spirit light, and one eye gone a little cloudy with age. Her
  barbels curl like an old woman's whiskers and **stay inside the glass**: nothing of the koi ever crosses the bowl. When she's thinking
  she turns a slow circle. When she laughs, bubbles.
- **Old-lady touches.** A pair of gold pince-nez clipped to the bowl's rim, over nothing in particular. A plain
  wooden comb tucked into the ruffle, where a bun would be. A brass collar fitting where the bowl sits on the neck,
  patched and re-soldered many times.
- **The body.** Tall and very upright, a made body: pilgrims and temple carpenters built it for her over the
  centuries, so no two parts match. Long white lacquered hands with visible joints, like a bunraku puppet's,
  and always perfectly still until they move.
- **Clothes.** A healer's long pale grey-white overcoat with a high collar, cut like a doctor's coat, over an
  indigo kimono. Cobalt tassels at the sash. Wave-crest (seigaiha) embroidery at the hems, and thin brass pipes
  running from the collar down the coat: the water that moves the limbs.
- **At her hip:** a gourd of pond water and a small rack of glass vials, each a different blue.
- **Water,** not frost: drops hang in the air around her, a ripple spreads under her feet, and the bowl's water
  catches the light. Never ice.
- Palette: the Water element colour (`#2F7BFF`, a deep sea-blue, kept well apart from Frost's ice-blue `#59BFFF`)
  for the glow; cobalt, white and slate for the body and coat; one orange accent, her cheeks. **No teal** in her
  own colour, though the pond she stands in is teal soul-fire, which is not hers.

in the temple. Clips: Idle (the koi drifting, a bubble now and then), Talk (the hands come up), and Lift (pulling

## History

- **The pond.** The temple on the hill was built around her pond, not the other way about. For three hundred years
  the sick climbed the hill, knelt at the water, and went down again well. The carpenters built her a body so she
  could treat the ones who couldn't kneel. She's been through four.
- **Mizore.** A herb-gatherer's daughter from the valley climbed the hill at twelve and refused to leave until
  she'd been taught. Asagi taught her for six years: needles, bone-setting, cold cloths, and when to say nothing.
  Then the girl went to the palace and became the court physician. Mizore calls her **"Teacher"**; Asagi calls her
  **"little sleet"**, because the girl was always cold and always clear.
- **The prince.** Kagetsune climbed the hill once, young and alone, to ask the oldest living thing he'd heard of
  how it was done. She told him the truth: that she'd never chosen it, that it was lonelier than dying, and that
  there was no secret worth having. He went down the hill and started looking for one. She counts that as hers.
- **The monk.** Utsusemi's monk stopped at her pond every spring on his roads, and fed her rice. He talked to the
  fish, not the body. She liked that.
- **The two guards.** Two of the Emperor's guards used to come up the hill to sit by the pond, away from the
  palace, and one of them played a bone flute while the other listened. She never spoke to them; it wasn't a
  doctor's business. She knew their faces. That was [Oni](Oni.md) and [Shirogane](Shirogane.md).
- **The Night.** She was on the hill. She heard the bells from across the valley, more and more, and then she
  heard them stop. Nobody came up the hill after that. Then the pond filled with teal fire, and a man dragged
  himself out of it, and died, and came back, and came back.
- **Since.** She catches him. Every time he dies, he comes back into her pond, and she is the one standing in it,
  up to the knees, with her hands under his arms. Souls that slip past the coffin on the way back with him land in
  her water, and she hands them to him as Water.

## How a Water soul reaches Oni

A soul that dies tired, and only wants to be clean of it, sinks. It drifts to the pond because the pond is where
Oni goes. She lifts it out and gives it to him: the tide that pushes the army back, waves that wash the arrows out
of the air, and a healer's hands.

## Voice

**In game:** voiced in ElevenLabs (`ASAGI` in `Tools/generate-voice.mjs`), designed from the prompt below. Her
clips get a light glassy ring (`FX`), as Hibiki's are levelled, so she sounds as if she's speaking from inside the
bowl. She has no coffin echo: she was never in it.

**ElevenLabs voice design prompt:** "An elderly woman of about eighty with a warm, unhurried, slightly bubbly voice,
clear diction, and a soft gurgle of laughter under it. Teasing and nosy, like a village grandmother who has decided
you're hers. Never cold, never hurried. Speaks as if from inside a small room."

Long, fond sentences. Water and fish words used plainly: pond, drift, sink, scales, shallows. Delivery tags:
`[warm]`, `[fond]`, `[teasing]`, `[nosy]`, `[a bubble of laughter]`, `[quiet]`, `[old]`. She is the one soul who is
never dry, and the only one who calls Oni a nickname with affection in it.

## Arc across runs

The hands under his arms in the pool, then the student she made, then the boy she was honest with, the monk who
fed her, the two guards on her hill, and at last the patient she's sending home, by name. Alongside it, the pond
talks: the bodies, the flute, the clear stone, the dawn. She ends the way she began, standing in the water, but by
then he's walking into it instead of being thrown.

## Freed (the ending)

She has no bell: she was never in the coffin, and nothing needs to go silent for her. Her beat is the dawn. When
the sun comes up over the arena and the pond on the hill runs clear, if `Asagi_Meet_Home` has played:

| Moment | Line |
|---|---|
| The dawn, the water clear | `Asagi_Dawn`: **Asagi:** "Go on, both of you. The pond will keep." |

## Keep consistent

- She is warm, nosy and fond, and never dry or clinical: that's Mizore's register, and the contrast is the point.
- She calls Oni **"little fry"** and **"flute boy"**. She uses **"Oni"** only from `Asagi_Meet_Home` on, and in
  the pond talks that follow it. "Dear" for anyone.
- She was never in the coffin, has no bell, and is never teal. Her water is sea-blue; the pool she stands in is teal
  because it's soul-fire, not hers.
- Water, never ice. Ice is Mizore's; Black Ice is the one place they meet.
- Her medicine is real-world plain, like Mizore's (she taught her): warm cloths, clean water, breathing, rest.
- She knows who Oni and Shirogane are and has from the start. She doesn't say so until `Asagi_Meet_Flute`.
- Akagane's "carp" always gets "Koi." Hibiki's "Great-Grandmother" always gets "Yes, dear."

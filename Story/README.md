# The Story of Ronin's Revenge

The story behind Ronin's Revenge: what happened, why everyone keeps coming back, how it ends, and how lines should
react as runs pile up, the way Hades' do. Each character has their own file. **Check a character's file before
writing a line, a scene or art for them**, and update it when something about them changes. [RELATIONSHIPS.md](RELATIONSHIPS.md)
maps every bond in the cast: what they call each other, which pairs already talk, and where lines are missing.
[BOSS_ARCS.md](BOSS_ARCS.md) is the plan (not yet in game) for the bosses' progressive story: what they say about
each other, keeping score, and the phase 2 rewrite.

## The betrayal

Emperor Kagetsune was mortal once, and afraid of it. His two finest blades were his guards, **Oni** and
**Shirogane**, sworn to him and to each other. "Oni" was the name the Emperor gave him: his demon at the gate.

In secret, Kagetsune had an armor forged that no smith should have made. It was black scale edged in bronze, hung
with bells, and built around a reliquary coffin he carried as the head of a great hammer. Every soul sealed in the
coffin would feed the armor and ring one of its bells. A man wearing enough of them could not die.

He called it a coronation. On the **Night of a Thousand Bells** he summoned his whole court, his army and the
village Oni was born in to the mountain shrine, and Oni escorted them there himself, proud to. Benibana danced for
the feast, her smoke curling through the hall until no one could lift a hand. Then the coffin opened.

The bells rang until there were too many to hear apart. Thousands of souls poured into the armor in a flood of
teal fire, and Kagetsune rose out of it colossal and deathless. With the last toll, the night stopped where it
stood. Dawn has not come since.

Shirogane saw the armor reach for Oni and cut the tether that was dragging his soul into it. Kagetsune's hammer
came down on her, and her soul went into the coffin with the rest. Oni's tore loose. To mock him, Kagetsune had the
new faces on his armor cast as snarling oni.

## Why everyone comes back

- **Oni** is free but has nowhere to go. His soul can't pass on while the armor holds everyone he led up the
  mountain, so every death pulls him back to the hill where he and Shirogane used to sit, playing the bone flute
  she gave him. An abandoned temple stands on it, and it is there he wakes, in a pool of the same teal soul-fire he
  tore himself out of: the game's hub (Hades' House, the pool his Styx). He walks its courtyard between runs: her
  flute on the altar (the Flute Songs), the memorial stones, the weapon rack, and the gate out. The title screen is
  the hill outside it. Each return
  wears a little more of his memory away. The koi of the temple pond, [Asagi](Asagi.md), is the one who pulls him
  out of the pool each time; she has from the first death, and a few runs in she says so.
- **The army and the bosses** are souls from the armor, sent out each night. The army walks in the bodies it died
  in on the Night of a Thousand Bells: rotted armour and bone, a faint teal ember of soul in each eye slit
  ([Army.md](Army.md)). When one falls its body crumbles to bones and ash and its soul is drawn back into the coffin
  and forged again, so the army is always full and the bosses remember every fight. The yurei of the Frozen Shrine
  are the souls whose bodies are gone, so the armor hasn't bothered to give them one.
- **The endless night.** The last bell froze the hour, and Utsusemi winds the clock that holds it there. The moons
  over the boss fights (blood, steel, spirit) belong to the bosses, not to Kagetsune.
- **Boons.** When a soldier dies, its soul sometimes slips past the coffin's pull as a Fire, Frost or Storm soul.
  It lends Oni its power because he is the only one who might set it free. Every boon is a soul stolen back. Once
  he has met Asagi, the tired souls that slip back *with* him sink into her pond and come to him as Water. Planned,
  not yet in game: souls that die still holding something come back too heavy for the pull, and
  [Oliver](Oliver.md) pounces on them and drops them at Oni's feet as Steel.
  Technique scrolls are pages of Oni's own sword arts, the parts of himself the dying took.
- **Teal fire.** The fire pouring from Kagetsune's chest is the same teal as Oni's spirit flames and wisps,
  because Oni tore himself out of it.

## Endings

Like Hades, the first win isn't the end. Kagetsune falls, but the coffin still holds enough souls to forge him
again, and Oni wakes on his hill. A clear frees a boss for good once their story lines have played (see each
character's file): Benibana's bell goes silent, the monk's clock stops, Shirogane lays down her blades. When all
three are free and Kagetsune falls again, he can't be rebuilt. The **true ending** is dawn: the stopped hour moves,
the sun comes up over the hill, Oni plays the flute one last time, and the second spirit wisp beside him is
Shirogane.

**As built:** freeing is recorded (`SaveData.freed`, [BOSS_ARCS.md](BOSS_ARCS.md)): each boss's defeat on the freeing win is her farewell, and a freed soul no longer answers Kagetsune's summons. The true ending doesn't wait on it yet, so beating Kagetsune plays it straight away: his bells fall
silent one by one and the souls rise, leaving Shirogane's soul chained to her bell. Oni cuts the chain, as she once
cut his tether; her bell rings once and goes silent, and she rises as herself. The stopped hour ticks on and the dawn
comes up over the arena while Oni plays the flute, and she becomes the wisp beside him (`GameManager.PlayDawn`,
`ShiroganeFreed`). The rebuilt-Emperor first win is for when runs carry
over.

**Dialogue, as built:** runs now carry over (`SaveData.cs`) and the reactive system below is in `Dialogue.cs`: boss
intros (`BossIntro`), phase breaks (`BossPhase`) and defeats (`BossDefeat`), the killing boss's last word
(`KilledOni`), Oni waking in the temple after a run (`HubReturn`) and the souls on their first boon screen of a run
(`SoulPickup`) pick from the conversations in the character files. Intros, defeats and soul greetings play as
Hades-style talk scenes (the speaker painted large, Oni opposite); a phase break gets a scene only when Oni has an
answer, otherwise its card speaks. The souls' `SoulPickup`, Duo, Legendary, jealousy, boss and
temple lines are wired and voiced ([Souls.md](Souls.md)); only their freed beats in the ending are not yet.

## Consistency rules

- The world is always night until the true ending. Nothing in the game shows a sunrise before it.
- Teal is soul-fire: Oni's flames, Kagetsune's chest, freed souls. Don't use teal for anything else story-wise.
- Bells mean sealed souls. A bell going silent means a soul set free.
- Every boss is a victim of Kagetsune as well as Oni's enemy. None of them serves him willingly any more.
- No sexualised backgrounds or framing for any character (see the art guide's Shirogane notes for the same rule
  in art).
- When a relationship changes or a line between two characters is added, update
  [RELATIONSHIPS.md](RELATIONSHIPS.md) as well as both characters' files.
- Lines marked **(in game)** in the character files must match `Line()` in the boss's script and
  `Tools/generate-voice.mjs` word for word.

## Dialogue design: reactive lines, the Hades way

## What Hades does

Hades has more than 21,000 voiced lines across about 30 characters, and players rarely hear the same conversation
twice in a row. The sources agree on the approach:

- **Write for situations, and let the game pick.** Greg Kasavin (creative director) described writing as many
  different contexts as possible in which a character could talk to you, then having the game choose the right one
  for the moment ([GeekDad, 2019](https://geekdad.com/2019/10/narrative-and-early-access-supergiants-greg-kasavin-discusses-hades-development/)).
- **The mapping is authored by hand.** Each line's link to the situation it answers was built manually, permutation
  by permutation ([Sean Z, interview with Kasavin](https://seanzwrites.com/posts/supergiant-games-greg-kasavin-interview/)).
- **Casting, recording and implementation are one pipeline**, covered in Kasavin and Darren Korb's GDC 2021 talk,
  [Breathing Life into Greek Myth: The Dialogue of Hades](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m5KJSAj4afg). Kasavin also
  pointed people to Chris Bratt's November 2020 video breakdown of the conversation system
  ([his post](https://x.com/kasavin/status/1329487797607043073)). The line count comes from Supergiant's own
  breakdown ([Nintendo Wire](https://nintendowire.com/news/2020/12/30/hades-has-over-300000-words-of-voiced-dialogue-heres-a-handy-breakdown-of-who-speaks-most-and-least/)).

The full research, read from the game's shipped Lua scripts, is in [DIALOGUE_RESEARCH.md](DIALOGUE_RESEARCH.md).
In short: every conversation is a record with **requirements** (lines that must have played, lines that must *not*
have played, run counts, recent kills or deaths, items held), a **priority** (story-critical conversations jump the
queue) and **play-once** flags. Short **repeatable** barks sit underneath, so a character always has something to
say. Whatever plays is recorded in the save, which is what lets a conversation chain over dozens of runs.

## Our version

**A conversation record** (`Conversation` in `Assets/Scripts/Dialogue/Dialogue.cs`; every one is in
`Dialogue.Lines.cs`).

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `Id` | Unique, e.g. `Benibana_Intro_Bell`; the voice clips are `Audio/Voice/<Id>.mp3`, then `<Id>_<n>` |
| `Speaker` | A boss, `Oni`, a soul (`Akagane`, `Mizore`, `Hibiki`, `Asagi`), `Poguru`, or `Duo` |
| `Event` | Which moment it answers (below) |
| `Lines` | The exchange, each line with its own speaker |
| `Tier` | `Priority` > `Story` > `Reactive` > `Repeatable`. Priority (major events) and Story play once |
| `Requires` | Conditions that must all hold (below) |
| `Cooldown` | Runs before a Reactive or Repeatable can play again |

**Requirements.** Runs started and won; deaths, to whom and by what; wins against a boss; lines heard and not heard
(which chain lines into a sequence), and runs since; a boon or element held; the boss felled or ahead; the stage;
**firsts**: anything ever had or met (an item, a relic, a kind of soldier, a soul, a boss, Asagi, a temple
unlock), when, and whether it is still news (`GotWithin`, Hades' `MaxRunsSince`).

**Picking a line.** When an event fires, collect every line for that speaker and event whose requirements hold,
keep only the highest tier, and play the first unheard line in authored order (or a random one among
repeatables, from a shuffle bag). Record it in the persistent save. Every event keeps at least one `Repeatable` line
with no requirements, so nothing ever goes silent; the exceptions are `Tutorial` (played by id) and `PoguruFirst`
(nothing new, nothing to say). Mid-fight barks (hurt, stagger, move callouts) stay short, repeatable and never
cut off a full line (`GameManager.OnBossSpoke`).

**Events.**

| Event | When |
|---|---|
| `BossIntro`, `BossPhase`, `BossDefeat` | Boss title card, phase card, boss falls |
| `KilledOni` | Oni dies to this boss |
| `HubReturn`, `SoulHub` | Oni wakes in the temple pool; a soul speaks when he has nothing to say |
| `SoulPickup`, `Legendary`, `Duo`, `BossFelled`, `BossAhead`, `SoulJealous` | A soul's boon screen |
| `AsagiTalk` | Asagi at the pool's rim |
| `Tutorial` | Poguru's lesson and warnings, played by id |
| `PoguruRound`, `PoguruSoul`, `PoguruBoss`, `PoguruScout`, `PoguruLowLife`, `PoguruBossAhead`, `PoguruStage` | Poguru's barks in a run |
| `PoguruFirst` | Something first had or seen: Poguru says what it is |
| `PoguruWake`, `PoguruTalk` | Poguru on the wake (his roasts) and at his nest (news first) |

Each character's file holds their sample lines for these events.
